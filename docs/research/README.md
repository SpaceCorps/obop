# obop research index

Snapshot date: 2026-10-01. Twenty-eight fact-checked research notes for obop ("one business, one platform"): an open-source business suite in Rust (backend) and Vite+ (frontend and tooling), with Odoo as the functional reference and a clean-room implementation policy. By the owner's rulings the project is Apache-2.0, completely free to self-host, and agentic first (CLI, API and MCP for every capability).

> **Banner: the research predates the owner rulings of 2026-10-01.** The research is complete and synthesised into [../PRODUCT.md](../PRODUCT.md) and [../DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md). The owner then ruled on licence, price, marketplace, agentic-first delivery, name, funding, first market, pin policy, "vp only" exceptions, publisher, hosting, support and calendar: OR-1 to OR-13, recorded as RATIFIED in [../DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) under "Owner rulings (2026-10-01)". **Where this index or any research note disagrees with those rulings, the DECISIONS.md "Owner rulings" section wins; where it disagrees with any other part of DECISIONS.md, DECISIONS.md wins.** The research files themselves are not rewritten: they stay as the evidence base, and [../DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) section 7 lists what not to repeat. Subjects now superseded in whole or in part:
>
> - **Licensing and governance (R17; also R24 s8, R26 C2-C4):** the AGPL core with an Application Exception, Apache-2.0-only SDKs, the `ee` tree, the licence-grant CLA with an OSI-forever pledge, the steward veto and the company plus EU non-profit split. Ruled: Apache-2.0 for the whole project, DCO sign-off, no CLA, SpaceCorps as publisher (OR-1, OR-10).
> - **Marketplace commerce (R24 s9-s11):** paid listings, payouts, take rate, partner tier, white-label. Ruled: a free, open plugin registry; the `obop` CLI searches, installs (version-pinned) or copies plugin source; community and verified tiers; no payments (OR-3).
> - **Pricing, funding and risk model (R26):** plans and prices, ARPA, seed and Series A, hiring gates, break-even, hosted-cloud and SOC 2/ISO plans. Ruled: no proprietary paid software, no price list for the software, no revenue model for it; self-funded, the team is the owner plus AI agents; SpaceCorps hosts nothing for others today, and a paid hosting service on dedicated Sliplane servers is planned, not offered (OR-2, OR-6, OR-11, OR-22).
> - **Go-to-market and team (R20):** per-organisation pricing, the 8-12 person team, the free hosted on-ramp and the seven-app first loop. Ruled: the first loop is cut to kernel, invoicing, accounting, German e-invoicing, CRM and sales, and basic stock; the dates stay (OR-13).
> - **Release and support (R27 C1-C8; also R24 s5.3 and R21):** the 5-year LTS on every fourth release, the 24-month deprecation floor, the monthly patch train and ring rollout. Ruled: an N-1 upgrade path and a 12-month deprecation window before 1.0, then a 2-year security-fix window from the first stable release (OR-12).
> - **Steward, trademark, support and legal pack (R28):** a foundation-held trademark, a support cost model, a Data Act switching contract and an SLA for a hosting company. Ruled: SpaceCorps holds the marks, the name is cleared first, no hosting (OR-5, OR-10, OR-11).
> - **Open versus paid (R25 B7; also R26 A2, R17 s11.2):** a closed, ratcheting paid list, operated and warranted services, the Assurance service. Ruled: everything is open and free (OR-2).
>
> Research words such as "open core", "Community", "Enterprise", "EE", "`ee`", "paid list", "ratchet", plan names, "LTS", "GA", "paid cloud" and "steward" describe the research or Odoo, not obop policy. Newly ruled and absent from the research: **agentic first, CLI for everything** (OR-4: every capability is a typed command in the `obop` CLI, the REST API and MCP, the web UI is a client at parity before the first stable release; see [../spec/cli.md](../spec/cli.md)), the pin policy (OR-8), the exactly-two exceptions to "vp only" (OR-9), Germany first (OR-7) and the kept calendar (OR-13). ## 1. Purpose and how to read this directory

This file is the entry point: it summarises each note, pins the verified stack facts, and collects themes, contradictions, open decisions and known weaknesses across all 28 files. Every note ends with "Implications for obop" (MUST / SHOULD / COULD), "Confidence & gaps" and a "Verification log"; read those three sections first in any file you depend on.
Where notes disagree, section 5 says which one supersedes. Files 23 (kernel and architecture decisions) and 24 (extension platform, except its licensing, marketplace-commerce and white-label parts) carry the binding technical resolutions; 26's commercial resolutions are historical (OR-2, OR-6); later notes generally correct earlier ones, and the owner rulings of 2026-10-01 outrank all of them.
Evidence tags used throughout: V = verified against a live primary source, S = secondary source (press, Wikipedia, vendor claim), I = inference or model, G/M = guess or recalled from memory, U = unverified. Model outputs (costs, timelines, person-months) are design targets, not measurements.
Owner constraint (2026-10-01): the owner fixed pnpm as the only package manager and the `vp` CLI (Vite+) as the only entry point for JavaScript/TypeScript tooling; npm, npx, yarn and bun are never used by obop. The public npm registry is still where pnpm fetches packages from, so registry facts in these notes (versions, package names) legitimately say "npm". The owner later ruled the pin policy ("latest" is the newest release past a cooldown of 7 to 14 days, or its `x.y.1` patch; OR-8) and that there are exactly two exceptions to "vp only": the nightly exit drill and `pnpm sbom` through the vp-managed pnpm (OR-9).

## 4. Cross-cutting themes

1. **The opening is the delivery contract, not the language.** Odoo's worst grievances (upgrades, open-core surprises, gated API, surprise pricing) are business-model choices that a Rust rewrite would reproduce; obop wins by opening everything, making upgrades a product feature, shipping an ungated API and charging nothing (no price list, OR-2). Rust is a correctness and operations argument: Odoo sizes at about 6 users per worker and realistic Rust gains are 3-10x per box (an inference; no multiple is published before a harness exists, DECISIONS D21); R26's modelled hosting share of year-3 revenue is historical (OR-6). ([03], [04], [05], [22], [26])
2. **Open accounting is the wedge and the largest cost.** Odoo CE ships "Invoicing" only; `account` is about 1.9 MB of model code (5-6x `sale`), and localisation (229 `l10n_*` modules) is the moat. Full double-entry accounting in the free codebase (no separate "open core", no paid accounting, OR-1, OR-2) is the acceptance test for the whole platform. ([01], [09], [20], [25])
3. **A metadata-first registry is the single source of truth.** Models, fields, views, access rules, computed-field graphs, the typed command catalogue (and with it the `obop` CLI, REST, OpenAPI and MCP tools, OR-4), webhooks, import mappings, docs and the semantic layer all derive from one runtime registry (typed Rust core plus tenant rows); Studio, custom fields and approvals are only possible if built-in behaviour is also metadata. ([02], [08], [15], [19], [23], [24])
4. **Modular monolith with an explicit integration contract.** Odoo's 400 auto_install bridges show glue works but creates gravity wells; obop uses ports, transactional events, a durable outbox and a dependency law (impl crates depend only on api crates) with hub ports (`DemandPort`, `QtyContributionPort`, `PostingPort`, `InvoiceableSource`) to keep bridges linear. ([01], [05], [23])
5. **Integrity lives in the database, not only in Rust.** Deferred balanced-entry trigger, posted-row immutability, lock dates, gapless legal numbers assigned at posting, hash chains, a unique quant key with `ON CONFLICT`, immutable done moves and minor-unit money; Odoo checks balance only in the ORM and tolerates duplicate quants. ([08], [09], [10], [22], [23])
6. **Compliance moves on the legal calendar, so it must be data.** E-invoicing mandates, CRA reporting (live 2026-09-11), the Data Act (2027-01-12), the PLD (2026-12-09) and EN 16931 code-list cycles (next 2026-11-16) change faster than release trains; localisation ships as signed, separately versioned, effective-dated packs on their own channel, with Germany first (RATIFIED, OR-7). Which of these duties fall on SpaceCorps, which hosts nothing for others, is for counsel (OR-10, OR-11; DECISIONS Q5). ([09], [17], [25], [28])
7. **Licence and governance are the trust product.** Cal.com, MinIO, Elastic and Redis show the cost of flip-flops. Ruled: the whole project is Apache-2.0, contributions carry a DCO sign-off with no CLA, nothing is paid, and SpaceCorps is publisher, copyright holder and trademark holder (OR-1, OR-2, OR-10). Historical research position, **superseded by OR-1, OR-2, OR-6**: an AGPL core with an Application Exception, Apache-2.0 SDKs, a licence-grant CLA with an OSI pledge, a closed and ratcheting paid list, and a company plus EU non-profit split. ([03], [05], [17], [26])
8. **No third-party code in the core's trust domain.** In-process Rust plugins would be unsafe (R17 also called them AGPL derivatives; that licence argument is superseded by OR-1 and the security argument stands); data holds only total, metered CEL expressions, code is Wasm (pinned wasmtime LTS, separate `ext` process role) or remote OAuth apps, and third-party UI runs in remote-dom or sandboxed iframes, never in the main JS realm. ([17], [21], [24])
9. **PostgreSQL facts constrain the architecture.** Database per business (not pool), no NOTIFY in business transactions (cluster-wide commit lock), staged COPY imports because RLS blocks `COPY FROM`, PG 17+18 support with 18-only features behind a capability flag, UUIDv7 keys, cells of about 250 tenant databases. Three spikes to run in month one are specified but not executed. ([08], [18], [22], [23])
10. **Agents are principals on the same command surface.** Ruled as "agentic first, CLI for everything" (OR-4): one typed command catalogue reachable through the `obop` CLI, REST and MCP, with the web UI a client at parity before the first stable release and a feature not done until it exists in CLI, API and MCP ([../spec/cli.md](../spec/cli.md)). MCP 2026-07-28 and OAuth 2.1 at the edge; dry-run with digest-bound plan ids, idempotency keys, approval policy and separation of duties; agents create drafts, humans post; a governed semantic layer instead of raw text-to-SQL; BYOK with a fully self-hosted profile, where Odoo 20 meters all AI through IAP credits. ([16], [21], [23])
11. **Vite+ 1.0 is a thin, days-old, vendor-exposed layer.** The components beneath it (Vite 8, Rolldown 1.x, Vitest 5, Oxlint, TypeScript 7) are stable, but the alias/pinning layer, 0.x bundled tools, missing eject and the Cloudflare transition call for exact pins, one config factory, a nightly exit drill with quarterly human review (the first of exactly two RATIFIED exceptions to "vp only", OR-9), a licence allow-list and a named owner of upstream triage. ([07], [26], [27])
12. **Scope discipline decides survival.** Odoo has 642 addon directories and about 7,500 staff; obop's first delivery is the smallest honest loop for one country, Germany: kernel, invoicing, accounting with tax, German e-invoicing, CRM and sales, and basic stock, with a stated slip order and the dates kept (OR-7, OR-13; DECISIONS D86). R20's free compliant-invoicing on-ramp and seven-app loop are superseded, and manufacturing, POS, eCommerce, payroll and multi-country tax stay out of the first delivery. ([05], [20], [23], [25])
13. **Capacity, not money, is the binding constraint (R26's funding theme, superseded by OR-6).** R26 modelled about EUR 10.8M peak funding, EUR 2.2M ARR at month 36 and break-even near month 64, with people cost dominating; that model, its ARR-gated hiring and its paid-cloud assumptions no longer govern anything. The project is self-funded, the team is the owner plus AI agents, there is no revenue model for the software (OR-2, OR-6, OR-11; a hosting service is planned, OR-22), and whether that team can meet the kept dates is a named risk (DECISIONS D84), mitigated by scope in the D86 slip order and never by moving the German date. ([26])
14. **Portability and interoperability are now legal and competitive duties.** Full data export in every install, no egress or switching charges (SpaceCorps charges nothing, and the Data Act's 2027-01-12 ban is a duty of whoever hosts), an Odoo-compatible importer with external ids and dry-run, a free generated API, and a 12-month deprecation window before 1.0 (OR-12; the 24-month floor the research proposed is withdrawn) turn Odoo's paid upgrade and API gating into obop's differentiator. ([03], [21], [28])
15. **Accessibility, i18n and offline are structural, not polish.** WCAG 2.2 AA as a release gate (every drag has a non-drag twin), logical CSS and RTL/CJK in CI, PO + ICU MessageFormat 1 as the single translation format, an explicit offline contract, and release engineering sized for a very small team: an N-1 upgrade path and a 12-month deprecation window before 1.0, then a 2-year security-fix window from the first stable release (OR-12). R27's 5-year LTS on every fourth release is withdrawn; whether the CRA's five-year support period reaches a free project is counsel's question (DECISIONS Q5). ([12], [19], [25], [27])

## 7. Known weaknesses of this research

Collected from the Confidence & gaps and Verification log sections; "about N confirmed claims" counts in the logs are approximate tallies.

**Method limits**
- No workstream had a general web-search tool; discovery was by known URLs and APIs. Blocked or unreadable sources (403, bot checks, JS-only pages) include G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, Reddit, Gartner, IDC, EUR-Lex, DGFiP economie.gouv.fr, BMF, NetSuite, SAP, Sage, eBay and the WIPO/EUIPO/TMview registers. Two fetched pages carried text aimed at AI summarisers; the authors ignored it (10, 24).
- Nothing was built or benchmarked. All obop timings, costs, capacity figures, FTE and person-month estimates, score weights and the 3-year funding model are models or design targets. The three specified spikes (NOTIFY contention, 250-database cell, UUID size), the CEL cost-limit spike, fspy caching around cargo, Wasm per-call overhead, template-database timing and compile-time budgets were not executed. 26's peak funding (EUR 10.8M) and break-even (M64) could not be reproduced independently from its own parameters (a rebuild gave about EUR 12.7M and M71); that model is historical since OR-6 (self-funded, no revenue model) and no plan depends on it.
- Crate and library quality was judged from metadata (versions, downloads, dates), not code review; several relevant crates are weeks to months old (`en16931`, `ksef`, `verifactu`, `underskrift`).

**Odoo facts**
- Enterprise behaviour and edition boundaries are inferred from absence in the public Community tree plus docs; Enterprise model names are from memory and Studio internals are docs-only. Odoo's exact GA day, Light User billing unit, IAP credit prices, BYO-provider AI option, Odoo.sh prices and the extended-support fee are unknown; prices are the EUR (Europe) view only.
- Company figures are press or CEO statements: revenue, EUR 10B valuation (not a transaction), headcount (7,500 vs 8,000+), users (28M vs 16M stated by the same founder), partners (4,454 directory vs 21,000+ claim), the 2025 EUR 36M loss (single source), contributors (304 via API vs 2,515 sidebar vs 4,301 anonymous-inclusive).
- Which features moved out of Community at Odoo 12/13 is only partly verified (payroll and coupons checked); OCA and other free "accounting for Community" apps were not surveyed.

**Stack facts**
- Rust 1.99.0 went onto the dist channel on 2026-10-01 (manifest Last-Modified 12:38 UTC); notes written before then record it as absent. Pin only a version the dist channel serves and that passes the waiting rule of D23. Vite patch level drifts (8.3.1 vs 8.3.2). Vite+ facts rest on the npm registry, GitHub and docs; no independent benchmarks, community sentiment, Cloudflare deal status, or confirmation that the repo was public during the BUSL window. The "exit drill" proves code portability, not Vite Task configuration.
- The Vite+ 1.x stability contract exists only in a pre-1.0 plan issue; no Q4 roadmap was found.
- PostgreSQL 19 GA date, RLS overhead, JSONB-versus-column cost and schema-per-tenant degradation are unmeasured; the Crunchy tenancy thresholds are a 2023 vendor blog; TechEmpower ratios depend on entry choice and hardware was not re-verified.

**Legal, regulatory and compliance**
- Nothing here is legal advice. The research listed these counsel items: the AGPL plus Wasm/exception boundary, CLA under EU law, CRA manufacturer/steward classification (the Art. 64(10) exemption does not literally cover the paragraph-2 fine tier; Art. 69(2) timing was not re-fetched), NIS2 scope for hosted cloud, sanctions scope for ERP services to Russia (EU Regulation 833/2014 provision not located), Data Act contract design, EAA national penalties, insurance (no premium data) and trademark clearance (EUIPO, UKIPO, WIPO, DPMA, INPI searches not performed). **Re-scoped by the owner rulings (DECISIONS Q2, Q5, Q16, Q20, Q26):** the AGPL/Wasm boundary and the CLA no longer apply (OR-1: Apache-2.0, DCO, no CLA), and NIS2 for hosted cloud, Data Act contract design and insurance for a hosted service fall away while SpaceCorps hosts nothing for others (OR-11). Still open for a free, non-hosted Apache-2.0 project with SpaceCorps as publisher: name clearance (OR-5), the CRA role of SpaceCorps (OR-10), the trademark policy, dependency licence compatibility, the DCO wording for AI-assisted commits, and the sanctions and data-protection duties of the free sandbox if one is ever opened (DECISIONS Q27). EAA national penalties and the sanctions scope are kept as research background (an inference: they bear mainly on third parties who host obop or sell services on it).
- Mandate dates after August 2025 rest largely on the VATupdate aggregator and press: France PA count and SME timetable details, Belgium royal decree date and e-reporting 2028, Spain's final ministerial order and B2B dates, Greece Phase B, Slovakia, Saudi waves, UK MTD, Malaysia, Brazil, Mexico and Turkey thresholds. Poland's KSeF penalties (VAT Act Art. 106ni) start 2027-01-01 under current law; on 2026-09-16 the Ministry of Finance announced on gov.pl a deferral to 2027-12-31 (penalties from 2028-01-01) that needs a statute and was not enacted by 2026-10-01: plan on the announced date, keep it flagged as pending legislation (a watch item, partial evidence). The EU Pay Transparency transposition status, PSD3 and the effect of the separate Nov 2025 Digital Omnibus proposal (COM(2025) 837) on the CRA are unverified. The AI Act high-risk dates rest on Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, identified on 2026-10-01; its corrigendum of 2026-09-29 was not read.
- Licence terms not read or not found: SKR03/04 (DATEV) and RGS chart licences, BAS (read; proprietary), NF525/BYCYB price and lead time, ZATCA onboarding, Peppol AP and PA certification lead times.

**Market and business**
- Analyst market sizes are internally inconsistent or gated (Mordor; Gartner/IDC unreadable); there is no verified SMB SaaS-sprawl figure, no public churn or NRR for Odoo, Frappe, Twenty, Supabase, Grafana, Metabase, n8n, Akaunting, Invoice Ninja or Holded, and willingness to pay for nonprofits and local government is inferred. Voice-of-customer evidence is thin (review sites, HN, GitHub issues; no first-person "why we left Odoo" cases).
- Vendor prices are regional snapshots; NetSuite, SAP Business One, Sage, Jira/JSM billing basis, Zoho One All-Employee, Frappe and Atlassian marketplace commissions and several aggregator prices were not retrievable.

**Coverage not done**
- US sales-tax rate engines, UK MTD specifics, Japan qualified invoices, Australia/Canada GST, IFRS 15/16 and consolidation standards (IAS 21, IFRS 10 for CTA and minority interests, marked unverified in 23), NetSuite OneWorld behaviour, native Odoo mobile features, Postgres HA tooling (Patroni/CloudNativePG maturity), 3PL/WMS depth, hands-on accessibility testing of Odoo, user research of any kind, and native-speaker review of the name in zh/ar/hi/id/tr/pt-BR/ja/ko.
