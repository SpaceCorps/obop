# 17 - Open-source licensing, governance and business models for obop

Research date: 2026-10-01. This is research, not legal advice. Items that need a lawyer are collected in section 12.

Evidence tags used below: **[V]** verified against a primary or near-primary source fetched today; **[S]** secondary source (Wikipedia, press, blogs); **[I]** my inference; **[M]** from memory and not re-verified.

## Scope & method

I fetched about 85 pages and documents on 2026-10-01: licence texts (GNU, Apache, MPL, FSL, BSL, ELv2, SSPL, n8n SUL), the full text of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (Cyber Resilience Act, CRA) plus the Commission's open-source page, FAQ and guidance announcement, GitHub LICENSE and README files, vendor pricing pages, foundation and funder pages (NLnet, Sovereign Tech Agency, ZenDiS/openDesk), and Wikipedia for history and funding figures. ## 1. Bottom line

1. The licence has to carry four jobs for obop: satisfy public-sector and funder "open source" gates, allow a third-party plugin ecosystem, resist a competitor taking the code and hosting it, and keep CRA exposure manageable. No single licence does all four.
2. Recommended: **AGPL-3.0 core with an explicit extension (plugin) exception, Apache-2.0 SDK/API/protocol crates, a commercially licensed enterprise layer, a CLA (licence grant, not assignment) with an irrevocable "OSI-licence-forever" pledge, and the trademark parked in an independent non-profit**. Monetise through managed cloud, certified partners, SLA support, maintained compliance packs and a narrow buyer-based enterprise tier. Details in sections 9 and 11.
3. Rejected as the primary licence: FSL/BUSL/ELv2/SSPL (fail OSI, fail most public-sector and grant gates, undermine the "open-source OS" positioning) and Odoo-style LGPL (weak protection against hosting competitors, and awkward for a statically linked Rust binary [I]).
4. The CRA is live for reporting since 2026-09-11. Once obop (or a company around it) sells or monetises the software it is a *manufacturer* with fine exposure; only a non-manufacturer legal person that systematically supports FOSS qualifies as a fine-exempt *steward*. This pushes toward a company plus foundation split from day one.

### 2.2 Mechanics that drive the obop decision

- **Plugins and the "combined program" test.** The FSF's position is that it depends on how the plug-in is invoked: fork/exec with no intimate communication is a separate program; dynamic linking or shared complex data structures make a single combined program, and then the plug-in must be GPL-compatible ([V] https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html). Odoo's Python add-ons run in-process; a Rust platform typically compiles everything into one binary. **A Rust-native extension model (in-process crates) would make every plugin a derivative of an AGPL core [I].** If obop wants proprietary third-party plugins under AGPL, the plugin boundary has to be out-of-process (HTTP/gRPC/webhooks) or sandboxed (WASM) *and* backed by an explicit additional permission. Twenty's LICENSE is a working template: AGPL plus a "Twenty Application Exception" (AGPL s.7 additional permission) that lets apps built on defined Application Interfaces carry any licence, while modifying Twenty itself still triggers full AGPL incl. s.13 ([V] https://github.com/twentyhq/twenty/blob/main/LICENSE).
- **AGPL s.13 is narrower than people assume.** The quoted trigger is "if you modify the Program". An unmodified AGPL deployment hosted by a third party owes no source. AGPL therefore does *not* stop a competitor from running stock obop as a service; it stops them from keeping *their changes* private ([V] AGPL text above). SSPL is the licence that tries to stop hosting, and OSI rejected it as a field-of-endeavour restriction (OSD6) ([V] OSI link above).
- **AGPL adoption cost.** Google's policy is that AGPL code "MUST NOT be used at Google" ([V] https://opensource.google/documentation/reference/using/agpl-policy). Large-corporate adopters and contributors may follow similar policies [I]. For an SMB-focused ERP this is a modest cost; for a developer-tool or infra component it would be severe.
- **Patents.** Apache-2.0 s.3 gives an express grant with termination for the party that sues; GPLv3/AGPLv3 s.11 has each contributor grant a royalty-free licence to its essential patent claims ([V] AGPL and Apache pages above). MIT has no express grant. The Apache-2.0 patent clause is FSF-compatible with GPLv3 but *not* GPLv2 ([V] https://www.gnu.org/licenses/license-list.html), which matters if any dependency is GPLv2-only.
- **Trademarks.** None of Apache-2.0, AGPL or FSL grants trademark rights; AGPL s.7 explicitly allows declining to grant them. Trademark is therefore the real lever (see 5.2).
- **Source-available cliff.** FSL and BUSL are not OSI licences. The Commission's CRA page speaks of "free and open-source software" and the steward concept is defined only for software "qualifying as free and open-source software" (CRA Art. 3(14), [V] section 7). Whether FSL-licensed code is "FOSS" for CRA purposes is doubtful [I] and should be checked by counsel.

### 2.3 Rust and front-end tooling specifics [I]

- Rust ecosystems default to MIT/Apache-2.0 dual licensing. An AGPL application can consume those freely. Use `cargo-deny` style policy gating from day one; GitLab does the same for its stack with License Finder and a legal-approval path for unknown licences ([V] https://docs.gitlab.com/ee/development/licensing.html).
- Vite+ is MIT ([V] https://github.com/voidzero-dev/vite-plus, https://viteplus.dev/), so the mandated front-end toolchain adds no licensing constraint to the chosen core licence. Transitive JavaScript dependencies (installed by pnpm from the npm registry) still need licence scanning (`pnpm licenses list`; obop uses pnpm only, owner constraint 2026-10-01). Vite+ 1.0 was released as stable on 2026-09-28 (v1.0.0, promoted from v1.0.0-rc.1 with no code changes; [V] https://github.com/voidzero-dev/vite-plus/releases/tag/v1.0.0).

### 3.2 Patterns

1. **Relicensing toward source-available was reversed or contested in 2 of the 4 big hyperscaler-era cases**: Elastic (2021 SSPL, 2024 AGPL added) and Redis (2024 SSPL, 2025 AGPL added). HashiCorp did not reverse and the fork (OpenTofu) persists under the Linux Foundation [V/S above].
2. **Every major relicense produced a fork within weeks to months**, and forks tend to end up in the Linux Foundation (OpenSearch, Valkey, OpenTofu).
3. **Two 2026 closures are not about hyperscalers**: MinIO (strategy shift to AIStor; archived 2026-04-25) and Cal.com (security rationale, 2026-04-14). The dominant risk for a community is now the steward withdrawing, not a hyperscaler taking.
4. **Copyleft-plus-commercial is the dominant ERP-adjacent pattern** (Metabase, Twenty, Grafana, Nextcloud, Odoo's inverse with LGPL).
5. **ERP is a less natural hyperscaler target than infrastructure** (long tail of localisation and customisation, services-heavy) [I]. The realistic "hyperscaler" for obop is a well-funded ERP vendor or a hosting provider running stock obop.

## 4. Contributors: CLA vs DCO

| | CLA (licence grant, not assignment) | DCO sign-off |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | Gives the steward rights to redistribute, and if drafted so, to relicense contributions; typical of corporate copyleft projects ([S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contributor_License_Agreement) | Contributor certifies right to submit under the project's licence; no extra grant ([V] https://developercertificate.org/) |
| Enables | Dual licensing, enterprise layer built on community code, later relicensing | Nothing beyond the project's existing licence |
| Cost | Friction; GitLab cited the CLA as a deterrent and Debian asked them to reconsider ([V] GitLab DCO post) | Minimal |
| Trust risk | MongoDB and Elastic (Jan 2021) used CLA-granted rights to move to non-open licences ([S] CLA article; that article dates MongoDB's move to 2019, but the SSPL was published 2018-10-16 and MongoDB withdrew its OSI submission in 2019, [S] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Server_Side_Public_License); "rug-pull" perception | Low |
| Mitigation | FSFE Fiduciary Licence (rights revert if steward abandons free-software principles); Ubuntu/Project Harmony variants that must keep licences in use at submission ([S] CLA article) | - |
| Who uses it in this space | Odoo (ICLA and CCLA in-repo), OCA (ICLA/ECLA before commit rights, [V] OCA CLA page), Grafana | GitLab (MIT core) |

**Reading [I]:** with an AGPL core and a proprietary enterprise layer you need the right to license community code commercially, so a DCO alone is not enough unless the enterprise directory accepts *only* company-authored code. Odoo and OCA contributors are already used to CLAs, so friction in this ecosystem is lower than in a developer-tool community. The trust risk is addressed by a public, irrevocable pledge in the CLA that the contributed code will always remain available under an OSI-approved licence.

## 5. Patents and trademarks: what to do

### 5.1 Patents
- Use Apache-2.0 for SDK, client libraries, protocol/schema definitions (express patent grant and defensive termination). Use AGPL-3.0 for the core (s.11 contributor grant).
- CLA should include a contributor patent licence equal to Apache-2.0 s.3 [I].
- Avoid any licence that imposes an unspecified patent covenant (e.g., bespoke source-available terms). The ELv2, FSL and n8n licences all contain patent grants with litigation termination ([V] respective texts) so this is not a differentiator between them.

### 5.2 Trademarks
- Trademark control is the only enforceable barrier to look-alike hosting under AGPL/Apache. Models: Frappe (descriptive use allowed, no use in product/company/domain names, [V] policy above); Tryton (registered EU and US by the foundation in classes 9, 37 and 42, [V] foundation page); Odoo's partner agreement forbids the word "Odoo" in partner company, product and domain names (per file 03).
- Register "obop" (EUIPO at minimum, classes 9, 35, 42) *before* public launch and publish an objective policy. The WP Engine dispute shows the cost of discretionary trademark leverage ([S] above).

## 6. Hyperscaler / free-rider analysis for obop [I]

| Threat | Likelihood | Licence effect | Better counter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hyperscaler offers managed obop | Low to medium (ERP is not a core cloud primitive) | AGPL: must publish *modifications* only | Trademark, partner network, SLA, compliance packs |
| Hosting provider runs stock obop and undercuts | High | AGPL does nothing; FSL would | Cloud operations advantage, pricing, certified partner programme, enterprise-only operational features (multi-tenant control plane, backup/DR tooling) |
| Competitor forks and closes | Medium | Copyleft prevents closing the fork | AGPL + CLA holder keeps the only commercial licence |
| Well-funded ERP vendor embeds obop in a proprietary suite | Medium | AGPL blocks; MIT/Apache would allow | AGPL core |
| Steward itself withdraws (MinIO, Cal.com pattern) | Medium over 10 years | Licence cannot prevent | Foundation-held trademark, escrowed source, public pledge |

### 7.2 What this means for obop [I unless tagged]

1. **Role split.** A company that sells support, cloud or an enterprise edition of obop monetises the software and is a *manufacturer* (Art. 3(13): "whether for payment, monetisation or free of charge"); the Commission's open-source page says only FOSS supplied in the course of a commercial activity is in scope, and non-monetised provision is not commercial [V]. A separate non-profit that systematically supports development of the FOSS without placing it on the market is the natural *steward* [I], with the Art. 64(10) fine exemption. A hybrid (one entity doing both) would be a manufacturer. This is a strong structural argument for a company + foundation split, but classification needs counsel.
2. **ERP is probably in the "default" category** (self-assessment, Module A): Annex III (important products, Class I and II) and Annex IV (critical products) do not list ERP or business suites [V OJ text]. Caveat: Annex III Class I lists "Identity management systems and privileged access management software" and "Network management systems", so an obop-shipped identity provider/SSO or PAM component should be checked by counsel [I].
3. **Security updates vs open-core gating.** Annex I Part II point (8) requires that security updates "are disseminated without delay and, unless otherwise agreed between a manufacturer and a business user in relation to a tailor-made product with digital elements, free of charge" ([V] OJ text, Annex I Part II(8); see also https://www.cyberresilienceact.eu/the-cyber-resilience-act/). The only carve-out is a tailor-made product agreed with a business user, which does not fit a generic enterprise tier. Design implication: **never gate security fixes behind the enterprise licence**, and ship fixes for every supported branch to all editions.
4. **SBOM is an engineering requirement.** Rust's `Cargo.lock` plus `cargo-cyclonedx`/SPDX generation, the `pnpm-lock.yaml` lockfile for the Vite+ frontend (`pnpm sbom --sbom-format cyclonedx` or `spdx`; check that any scanner reads pnpm's two-document lockfile, otherwise it reports no dependencies without failing), and signed artifacts are cheap to add at project start [I].
5. **Vulnerability disclosure.** Publish a security policy, a CVE-numbering-authority route or equivalent, and a 24h/72h/14-day internal runbook. Sovereign Tech Resilience's CRA compliance service (EY Consulting as provider) is aimed at open-source projects ([V] https://www.sovereign.tech/programs/resilience); eligibility for an ERP is unclear.
6. **Support period = LTS policy.** The 5-year minimum maps to Tryton-style LTS every fifth series. Odoo's yearly breaking major versions (see file 03) may be hard to square with this rule for a vendor selling the product in the EU [I].
7. **Hosted cloud.** Pure SaaS may fall outside the CRA but inside NIS2 as a cloud/digital service provider depending on size: Recital 12 of the CRA says cloud services designed outside a manufacturer's responsibility are outside the CRA, that Directive (EU) 2022/2555 applies to cloud computing services including SaaS, and that providers that are medium-sized or larger fall within that Directive [V OJ text, Recital 12]; the "remote data processing" test may still pull it in [V FAQ]. Lawyer item.

**Decision:** hybrid. The non-profit should be European (Belgium, Netherlands, Germany and Switzerland are all precedents) and lawyer-selected. It holds the trademarks and domains; the company holds a non-exclusive, non-discriminatory trademark licence like everyone else's (partners, hosts) under a published policy. Board of the foundation includes independent and community seats and a veto over any relicensing of the AGPL core to a non-OSI licence. Thresholds for transferring further assets (core copyright licences) can be defined later.

### 11.2 Recommended structure

- **Core and first-party apps:** AGPL-3.0-or-later (counsel to decide between "-only" and "-or-later"; ERPNext uses "-only", Tryton "-or-later").
- **Extension exception:** AGPL s.7 additional permission for modules that interact only through documented, versioned extension interfaces (HTTP/gRPC, webhooks, WASM host ABI), modelled on Twenty's Application Exception, with the explicit rule that modifying the core itself remains full AGPL.
- **Permissive edge:** Apache-2.0 for SDKs, client libraries, API/protocol/schema definitions, extension templates (as Twenty does with MIT). Plugin authors who link only these never touch AGPL.
- **Enterprise layer:** separate crates/packages (or a `ee/` tree with per-file markers), commercial licence, built into separate artifacts (Metabase ships distinct binaries per licence). **Rule:** no security fixes, no data-portability or export features, and no core ERP workflow gated; gate SSO/SAML/SCIM, advanced audit and retention, multi-tenant control plane, air-gap packaging, white-label, SLA tooling (consistent with Twenty and Metabase gates).
- **CLA:** licence grant (not copyright assignment, since several EU jurisdictions restrict assignment [M]), including patent licence and a public, irrevocable OSI-licence-forever pledge or FSFE-style reversion.
- **Governance:** company plus EU non-profit holding trademark (section 9).
- **Pricing anchor:** Odoo Standard is EUR 19.90/user/month (yearly billing; a 12-month discount for initial users, regular yearly price EUR 24.90) for all apps on Odoo Online, so obop Cloud should differentiate on ownership, portability and compliance warranty rather than headline price [I].

### 11.3 Pros and cons of the recommended path

| Pros | Cons / risks |
|---|---|
| OSI-approved: qualifies for NLnet, STF (for components), openCoDE/ZenDiS-style buyers | AGPL deters some corporate adopters (Google policy) |
| Same licence family as Metabase, Grafana, Nextcloud, Twenty; proven commercial pattern | AGPL does not stop unmodified hosting; must rely on trademark, operations, partners |
| Protects against proprietary forks | Plugin-boundary ambiguity is a legal risk until tested; all in-process Rust plugin designs become derivative |
| Pledge and foundation reduce "rug-pull" (MinIO, Cal.com) fears | Governance overhead and cost from day one |
| CRA split gives a steward route for the community project | Hybrid entity structure is novel and needs legal design |

### 11.4 Key risks and triggers

1. Hosting competitor undercuts: mitigation is partner programme and compliance SLA, not licence changes. **Trigger to revisit licence:** a sustained hosted competitor without contributions; even then respond with an additional service-level offering first (Elastic and Redis show licence retreats were costly).
2. Plugin ecosystem stalls because of AGPL fear: publish the exception early, and run a legal opinion for plugin authors.
3. AI-driven vulnerability discovery (Cal.com's stated reason for closing): invest in secure-by-default architecture, fuzzing, memory-safe Rust, and the CRA process instead of secrecy.
4. VC-driven relicensing pressure: embed the pledge and foundation veto before any priced round.
5. Trademark governance abuse perception: objective policy, no revenue-share demands on hosts [WP Engine].

## Implications for obop

**MUST**
1. **MUST** license the core as copyleft OSI software (recommended: AGPL-3.0 family) and **MUST NOT** use FSL, BUSL, SSPL or ELv2 for the core; they fail OSI, public-sector and grant gates and cloud the CRA "FOSS" treatment.
2. **MUST** publish an extension/plugin exception plus an Apache-2.0 SDK/API layer before soliciting third-party modules; **MUST** design the plugin boundary (out-of-process or WASM) with licensing in mind, because in-process Rust crates become AGPL derivatives.
3. **MUST** require a CLA (licence grant) with an irrevocable OSI-licence-forever pledge for the core; **MUST NOT** rely on DCO alone if any community code will sit beneath a commercial licence.
4. **MUST** register the trademark and publish an objective trademark policy (Frappe/Tryton style) before launch; **MUST NOT** use trademark as discretionary leverage over hosts (WP Engine lesson).
5. **MUST** treat CRA readiness as a product requirement now: SBOM generation in CI (Rust via `cargo-cyclonedx`/SPDX, JavaScript via `pnpm sbom`), signed releases, a published security policy and coordinated-disclosure route, an internal 24h/72h/14-day reporting runbook (Art. 14 is live since 2026-09-11), and a support-period/LTS policy of at least five years.
6. **MUST** keep security fixes out of any paid gate.
7. **MUST** engage counsel for the items in section 12 before the first public release.

**SHOULD**
8. **SHOULD** structure as a company (manufacturer, commercial edition and cloud) plus an EU non-profit (steward of the community project, trademark holder), with a veto over moving the core off OSI licences.
9. **SHOULD** monetise in this order: managed cloud, certified partner programme and SLA support, commercially maintained compliance/e-invoicing packs with update-SLAs, and a narrow buyer-based enterprise tier (SSO/SCIM, audit, control plane, air-gap, white-label).
10. **SHOULD** keep all core ERP functionality (accounting, sales, purchase, inventory, manufacturing, HR, CRM, projects) in the AGPL edition to avoid the Odoo open-core backlash that produced OCA.
11. **SHOULD** apply to NLnet Restack (next deadline 2026-11-03) and the Sovereign Tech Fund for discrete, reusable Rust libraries, not the whole ERP; **SHOULD** monitor the EU Open Source Strategy's stewardship toolkit and Maintenance Instrument.
12. **SHOULD** add licence gating in CI (cargo-deny plus pnpm licence scanning, `pnpm licenses list`) and record every third-party licence decision.

**COULD**
13. **COULD** adopt an FSFE-style fiduciary reversion in the CLA for maximum contributor trust.
14. **COULD** open a curated extension marketplace with signed packages after the governance basics are in place; set the revenue share only after checking Odoo Apps and Frappe actuals.
15. **COULD** pursue a hosted-service differentiation (multi-tenant control plane, regional sovereignty hosting) that AGPL users cannot easily replicate.
16. **COULD** join the Eclipse ORC working group or similar CRA communities to reuse steward policy templates ([V] https://orcwg.org/ exists and has a Cyber Resilience Act section; template contents not verified).
