## 0. Summary

A customer who runs an obop instance for its own people can show them its own name, not "obop". One record, `instance_brand`, holds the name, a slug, the logo, a sign-in title per language, a sign-in message per language and (from S2) one switch. Every server-side place that shows the product name to an end user reads that record through one reader. The phone app is the one exception (section 1). An instance that sets nothing renders its pages as today, with two exceptions that arrive in S2: two sentences are reworded for every instance, and the back-office index document answers 503 while the store cannot be read, where today it is served from the file (section 12 says which slices are byte for byte identical, section 13 which outputs).

S3 adds more for every instance, and none is in that list of S2: while the store cannot be read, `initialize` over HTTP, the discovery documents and the OpenAPI document answer 503 where they were served from code, a Slack message is refused where it was sent signed `obop`, and the refusal of the Drive browser for a Google file of a kind it cannot bring in names no product (section 12, "S3, what an instance that sets nothing also sees"; docs/ISSUES.md PS3-1, PS3-4 and PS3-10, each the owner's to decide).

- One record, three typed commands (`get`, `configure`, `clear`) on the CLI, REST and MCP and a tab in Customize, changed only by a person who holds `platform.admin`, through one wrapper. `configure` merges the fields it is given over the stored row, with an explicit way to clear each field (sections 3 and 4).
- The owner answered the 14 items of Q71 on 2026-11-05 (relayed from his card; section 15.3): items 1 to 13 are answered, item 14 (licence carriers in a store binary and the embedded back-office view) is open. The owner declined an operator lock when asked about the free sandbox; the risk is stated in sections 14 and 15.1, and the spec extends the statement to a hosted plan as its own, not as part of the answer.
- Stored as a record, not as environment variables, because it is a command, it has a logo, several server processes must agree and a change must be audited (section 4).
- It defers to what exists: the instance theme owns the accent, the `site.*` keys stay the explicit override of the public site, `OBOP_MFA_ISSUER` stays an explicit override of the authenticator label, and `get` shows which source won for every value (section 5).
- Server-rendered pages take the record through a reader with a two-second process cache. The back office, which must show the name before anyone has signed in, gets a small public subset injected into its page, the way `OBOP_DEFAULT_LANGUAGE` is today. Both widen what is public, which is why section 15 names the records they change and lists the public read doors found by reading the routes and files cited there; the scans of section 13 only keep their closed lists of files closed (sections 4 and 6).
- Five sentence families of the OAuth pages name obop, and German, Finnish and Swedish attach the name to compounds and case endings. With a brand set they switch to neutral wording that names no product, in all seven languages (section 7), and a client that is named like the brand gets one more fixed sentence (section 10).
- The OAuth and signing pages keep their content security policy. The logo there is inline SVG built by the server from a strict subset, never an image or a style (section 8).
- What the record cannot touch is fixed and listed: the security statements of the consent page, the wire names, the licence and NOTICE text, any attribution (sections 10 and 11).
- Delivery is four slices, S1 to S4, sized in section 12 from one ledger (sections 6 and 6.1). S1 alone is not a customer-ready cut. Tests, including a scan that fails when a literal `obop` is added to a page that should use the record, are in section 13.

## 2. The record

One model, `instance_brand`, in the built-in module `platform` (the Customize app) beside `setting` (`K/obop-pkg/src/installer.rs:2418`) and `sso_provider` (`K/obop-pkg/src/sso.rs`). One row, id `instance`. No row means no brand.

**One predicate.** A brand is *set* when the row exists. That is the only whole-brand test. It decides the neutral wording (section 7), the icon link and the policy addition (section 8), the generated tile and the shell rewrite (section 4). Each field is then read on its own: a field that fails the read check of section 4 falls back to its default for that field, and the rest of the brand stays. A row that names `obop` is not special: it renders the name obop with the generated tile unless it carries an artwork, and nothing it holds is dropped without a word. `clear` restores the stock look.

Every field has data class `none` (the code's `DataClass::None`, "explicitly not personal data", the class `setting.value` has; CONVENTIONS 5 lists no row for it) and permission group `*` (`ANY_GROUP`, `K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:55`). The group matters: `shape()` withholds a key from a principal that does not hold the field's group (`K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:1304-1307`), and the signed-in back office of a person who is not an administrator reads `hide_product_docs` through `get`. `sso_provider` gives its fields `ADMIN_GROUP` (`K/obop-pkg/src/sso.rs:19-20`) because only administrators read it; that is not copied. Who may write is decided by the commands (section 3), not by the field group. Every field except `hide_product_docs` is public by design: pages before sign-in show it (section 4), so none may hold personal data; the field descriptions and the form say so. `hide_product_docs` is not shown before sign-in; any signed-in principal reads it through `get`. `<lang>` is each of `en`, `de`, `fr`, `pl`, `fi`, `sv`, `es` (`K/obop-oauth/src/page.rs:16`); a test ties the model's language fields to that list, so an eighth language is a deliberate model change.

| Field | Kind and limit | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `name` | text, 1 to 40 characters | The product name. Required: a row without one is not stored. Character policy below |
| `slug` | text, up to 30 | `a-z`, `0-9` and `-`, starting with a letter, no trailing `-`. Empty means derived from `name` (below). The stem of the recovery-codes file and the MCP `serverInfo.name` |
| `title_<lang>` (7) | text, up to 60 | The line beside the logo on the OAuth and signer pages, in that language. Empty means the English title, then `name`. Same character policy as `name`. The back office does not show it: its sign-in and tab show `name` (section 6) |
| `message_<lang>` (7) | text, up to 240 | One plain-text message under the lead of the sign-in screens (on the back-office sign-in, under its hint line) in that language. Empty means the English message, then none. Character policy below; shown escaped, no links |
| `logo_svg` | longtext, 16 KiB in and 16 KiB canonical | The logo and the tab icon, one artwork. Input is an SVG file; what is stored is the canonical text the parser of section 8 re-wrote, never the input. Empty means the generated tile |
| `hide_product_docs` | bool (added in S2) | Hide the Brand book and the Guide in Customize, the account menu and the command palette (section 6). Nothing reads it before S2, so the field and the flag arrive with S2 |

Characters count as Unicode code points, as D90 does, in the NFC text; a text of more than eight times its limit, counted before it is normalised, is refused as too long without being normalised. There is no stored logo hash. The *version* of the logo, `v`, is the first 16 hex digits of the SHA-256 of the exact bytes the logo route serves (the artwork re-serialised from the stored text when that text parses under the SVG reader of section 8, else the generated tile drawn from the name and the tile accent). One function of `obop-brand`, `logo::version`, computes it for every snapshot the `Reader` builds, so the `ETag` and the `?v=` of the icon URL always follow what is served, also when a theme change alone alters the tile. `v` is empty when no brand is set. The icon URL is `/api/brand/logo.svg?v=<v>` in every case.

No colour field: the accent stays the instance theme's (section 5). No `logo` for dark mode: one artwork, so a logo that must work on both light and dark backgrounds uses explicit colours (section 14).

**Character policy** (one allow-list for `name`, `title_<lang>` and `message_<lang>`, applied by `configure`; the reader applies only the safety subset of section 4). Text is normalised to NFC before the checks and stored in NFC. A character is allowed only if its Unicode general category is one of:

- a letter (`Lu`, `Ll`, `Lt`, `Lm`, `Lo`) whose Script_Extensions holds a real script, not only Common or Inherited. The mathematical alphanumerics (script Common, no extensions) are out; the Japanese prolonged sound mark U+30FC (category `Lm`, extensions Hiragana and Katakana, as remembered and not re-fetched) is in, so a name such as `株式会社ムーヴ` passes;
- a mark (`Mn`, `Mc`) directly after a letter, a digit or another mark, at most three marks in a row, for the scripts that need them;
- a decimal digit (`Nd`);
- the ASCII space U+0020 only, never leading, trailing or doubled (every other `Z` category is out, so no no-break space or ideographic space);
- punctuation: for `name` and `title_<lang>` the ASCII `- . , & ' + _ ( ) !` and nothing else (the apostrophe is the only quote mark); for `message_<lang>` any punctuation category (`P*`), currency symbols (`Sc`) and the ASCII `+` (category `Sm`, otherwise out), so that every mark a name may hold is allowed in a message too.

Whatever the category, a character with the Unicode property Default_Ignorable_Code_Point is refused. That covers the zero-width characters, the soft hyphen, the variation selectors, the tag characters U+E0000 to U+E007F and the Hangul filler; the bidirectional controls and the other format characters (`Cf`), controls (`Cc`), private use (`Co`), unassigned (`Cn`) and surrogates are refused by category. `<` and `>` are not allowed in any field. `name` and `title_<lang>` start with a letter or digit.

*Forms that are refused in all three fields*, checked on an NFKC-folded copy of the text from which the combining marks (`Mn`, `Mc`, `Me`) are removed, so that the fullwidth forms of the characters below are caught as their ASCII originals and a combining mark cannot break a run. The characters `:` `/` `\` and `@` (a message would otherwise take them as punctuation). A host-like run: a run of characters other than the space that contains a `.` with at least two letters or digits directly before it and at least two directly after it, when at least one of those two groups contains a letter, such as `shop.example`, `example.com`, `mohv.se` or `v2.beta`; a dot between groups of digits only is a date or a time and passes (`24.12.2026`, `03.11.2026`, `10.30`, `8.30-17.00`); legal-form suffixes and abbreviations pass, because their parts are single letters or the dot ends the run (`Inc.`, `e.V.`, `z.B.`, `S.A.`, `sp. z o.o.`). And a run of 7 or more digits of category `Nd`. This is a lint of the obvious forms of a link, an address and a phone number. It is not a guarantee: a message can still say where to go in words, a host name with a one-letter part (`a.io`) passes, other full stops (the ideographic and the Arabic one) are the punctuation of their languages, and the pages do not turn text into links.

*One script.* For `name` and `title_<lang>` the set of scripts of the letters must be a subset of one of these sets, the allowance of the Highly Restrictive level of Unicode UTS #39: a single script; Latin, Han, Hiragana and Katakana; Latin, Han and Bopomofo; Latin, Han and Hangul (Common and Inherited are neutral). A name in Han, Hiragana and Katakana only, such as `株式会社ムーヴ`, is a subset of the second set and passes; a name that mixes Latin and Cyrillic letters is refused; digits and the listed punctuation are script-neutral. The rule is not applied to messages. It does not catch a whole-script look-alike (an all-Cyrillic name that reads as a Latin one), and there is no protected-name list for third parties such as the Microsoft and Google names that the pages show; both are limits (section 14).

The tables the check needs (normalisation, general category, script extensions, default-ignorable) come from the ICU4X crates `icu_normalizer` and `icu_properties`, which are already in `Cargo.lock` (`Cargo.lock:859`, `Cargo.lock:882`) next to the `icu_collator` and `icu_locale` that `obop-commands` depends on (`K/obop-commands/Cargo.toml:24-25`); they become direct dependencies of `obop-brand`, and their licence (Unicode-3.0) is checked against the dependency rule when they are added, and is not verified here. One more crate becomes a direct dependency of `obop-brand`: `sha2` 0.11, already in `Cargo.lock`, which computes `v` (section 2; licence `MIT OR Apache-2.0`). No other crate is added: the case comparison of `consent.same_name` is the fold of section 10 (ignorable characters and white space removed, NFKC, then `str::to_lowercase`), not `icu_casemap` (which is not in `Cargo.lock`), and the tests that need generated input use a fixed-seed generator written in the test, because no property-test crate is in `Cargo.lock` either.

**The slug.** Derived from `name` when `slug` is empty: decompose (NFKD), drop marks, lowercase, keep each run of `a-z` and `0-9` and join the runs with `-`; if the result starts with a digit, put `i-` in front; cut it to 30 characters and trim a trailing `-`. If nothing is left (a name with no Latin letters or digits, such as one in Cyrillic or Han) the slug is `instance`, so it never falls back to `obop`; two such instances share it in `serverInfo.name` and in the recovery file, and an operator who wants a readable slug sets `slug`.

**The generated tile.** With a name and no `logo_svg`, the server draws a tile: one glyph on a rounded square, with the contrast rule the public site already applies (`K/obop-site/src/seo.rs:62-81`). The glyph is the first letter or digit of the name, one scalar value, uppercased with the full uppercase mapping of the standard library (`char::to_uppercase`, which has no simple, one-to-one mapping) and only when that mapping gives a single scalar, so `ß` stays `ß` and does not become `SS`, and a Greek letter with an iota subscript such as U+1FB3, whose full mapping is two scalars, stays as it is. If the name holds no letter or digit (a row written around the command: the safety subset of section 4 does not require one) the glyph is `O`, the first letter of the default name. The public site's own favicon tile is separate code that uppercases the first alphanumeric with `to_uppercase()`, which can expand, and draws `S` when there is none (`seo.rs:67-73`); it is not changed, so the shop's tile can differ from the brand tile for the same name (a limit, section 14). The *tile accent* is the theme's stored accent when it is a valid `#rrggbb` other than obop's default violet `#6d3fd0` (`UI/src/theme.ts:16`, `UI/src/tokens.css:76`), else a neutral slate, `#495670` (the Slate preset, `UI/src/theme.ts:22`). That holds when the theme stores no accent and also when it stores exactly `#6d3fd0`, because the Theme tab's "Set as the default for everyone" saves the whole resolved theme, default accent included (`BO/src/Customize.tsx:249,334`, `UI/src/theme.ts:25-26`): the sign-in screens then show the theme's violet and the tile stays slate. A violet chosen on purpose is treated the same. The rollout therefore sets an explicit accent (section 14). The tile's *fill* is `theme::resolve(tile accent).light.fill` and its glyph colour is `.light.on_fill` (today `K/obop-site/src/theme.rs:217-224`). That search moves a light accent until the contrast rules hold (`resolve_mode`, `theme.rs:183-214`), so the fill can differ from the stored hex; for the slate the test pins the value the search returns: the fill is `#495670` itself and the glyph colour is `#ffffff` (verified against `K/obop-brand/src/tile.rs` and its test in the code of pull request 69, merged as 10ed0bc9). That function lives in `obop-site` today, and `obop-site` depends on `obop-commands` (`K/obop-site/Cargo.toml:12`), which will depend on the leaf crate that draws the tile, so the tile could not call it without a dependency cycle. S1 therefore moves the pure colour maths of `theme.rs` (`resolve`, `parse_hex`, `to_hex`, `contrast`, the constants and `card_tints`; standard library only) with its unit tests into `obop-brand` as `obop_brand::theme`, and leaves `obop-site/src/theme.rs` as `pub use obop_brand::theme::*;`. Its callers (`seo.rs:66`, `assets.rs:11`, `settings.rs:110,230,300`, `tests.rs:687`) compile unchanged, the favicon tile of the shop keeps calling the same function, and the pins of section 13 show that the shop's bytes did not move. The tile is a template of the server with one escaped glyph; it is not read by the SVG reader of section 8 (which refuses `<text>` in stored artwork) and has its own test. The template is a view box of 64 by 64 with a rounded square (`rx` 14) filled with the tile fill and one `<text>` at x 32 and y 44, in Georgia (serif), size 36, weight 700, centred, filled with the glyph colour; the bytes the logo route serves carry no `width` or `height` on the root. It is the "M" placeholder for MOHV, and no file is needed for it. Swapping to the real logo later is one flag with one file, and it changes nothing else of the row: `obop admin brand configure --logo-file logo.svg` (section 3).

| `instance_brand.configure` | `obop admin brand configure [--name N] [--slug S] [--title LANG=TEXT]... [--message LANG=TEXT]... [--logo-file F or --clear-logo] [--from FILE] [--dry-run]` | `/api/instance_brand/configure`, `instance_brand__configure` | Merges the fields it is given over the stored row, validates, writes and audits in one batch. A refused value names the field and the rule, and nothing is stored. S2 adds `--hide-product-docs on or off` |
| `instance_brand.clear` | `obop admin brand clear [--dry-run]` | `/api/instance_brand/clear`, `instance_brand__clear` | Deletes the row; every surface is as today. With no row it answers `{"cleared": false}` and writes no event |
| (CLI shortcut) | `obop admin brand check FILE.svg` | `configure` with `dry_run` and only `logo_svg` gives the same refusals for the artwork | Runs the SVG reader of section 8 on a file with no database, like `obop modules check FILE`, and prints `v` or the refusals with their reasons (at most 20, section 8). It checks the artwork only and needs no name (a real dry run on an instance with no row also refuses the missing name), so there is no capability that only the CLI has: REST and MCP give the same refusals for the artwork |

**`configure` has one rule on all three surfaces.** The CLI, REST and MCP call the same function, `brand::apply`, which merges by supplied field:

- A field that is not supplied keeps its stored value. The first call needs `name`. So the one-flag logo swap `configure --logo-file logo.svg` replaces the artwork and leaves the name, titles and messages alone, and `configure --title sv=Logga in` changes one title. Only the supplied fields are checked, each by the whole policy of section 2, also when the value equals the stored one (which is why a release must not tighten the policy against the golden names of section 13). A stored field that is not supplied is kept as it is stored, one that would no longer pass the policy and a key that this binary does not know (a newer binary's) included.
- Each field has an explicit clear value. A text field (`slug`, `title_<lang>`, `message_<lang>`) is cleared by an empty string (the CLI `--title sv=` with nothing after the sign); the artwork by `logo_svg: ""` (the CLI `--clear-logo`); the switch is set by an explicit true or false (the CLI `on` or `off`). A cleared field is removed from the row, not stored empty. `name` cannot be cleared, only replaced; `clear` removes the row. A stored value that is not text (a row written around the command) is read as the word `not_text` where `changes` and the log show it (the artwork as `unreadable`), and any supplied value, an empty one included, replaces it and counts as a change, also a text that is the word `not_text` itself; the empty text therefore repairs such a row.
- `dry_run: true` (the CLI `--dry-run`) does all the checks, writes nothing, logs nothing, takes no lock, and answers what would happen: for `configure` the `changes` and the `refusals` (below), for `clear` whether a row would be removed. D80 asks a dry run with a digest-bound `plan_id` of a mutating command; this design has the dry run and no `plan_id`, because that is the approval path of an agent and no agent may run these commands (15.1, Q71 item 12, answered yes).
- It is idempotent by construction: the same values twice change nothing and write no event. That holds for a valid artwork that is stored in a non-canonical form too: it is the same logo with the same `v`, so sending it again changes nothing, and the row keeps its stored text unless another field of the call changes. That is what the idempotency key of D80 is for, so none is added (15.1, Q71 item 12, answered yes).
- `brand::apply` takes the cross-process lock `Backend::lock("brand.write")` itself (`K/obop-storage/src/lib.rs:385-389`; a lock of its own, as `auth.mfa` is, `K/obop-auth/src/lib.rs:312-315`) and does the read, the merge and the write under it. It does not rely on the dispatcher's transaction lock (`dispatcher.txn`, `K/obop-commands/src/shared.rs:17,164-174`) because the CLI path has no dispatcher, and `Backend::lock` is not re-entrant, so a second key avoids taking one key twice. Whoever calls it, the dispatcher or the CLI, two changes at once cannot lose each other.
- `--from FILE` is a CLI convenience: it reads the `[record]` table of a TOML file, whose keys are the writable fields of the record (`name`, `slug`, `title_<lang>`, `message_<lang>`, `logo_svg` and, from S2, `hide_product_docs`), and sends them as the fields of one call. It ignores a `[phone]` table (the phone-only values of the same file, section 1) and refuses any other table, any key outside a table, any key inside `[record]` that is not one of those fields, the declared answer fields (`dry_run`, `set`, `v`, `effective`, `fallbacks`, `changes`, `refusals`, `cleared`) included, any value that is not text, and a file with no `[record]` table. Each reason names the key or the table and the rule, or, for a file that is not valid TOML, the line and the parser's own message cut to printable ASCII, and none holds a value of the file. A flag may be given with `--from` for a field that the file does not set, and is added to the same call; a flag for a field that the file also sets is refused by the field's name. It serves the exception of section 1.
- On REST and MCP `configure` refuses, by name, any input key that is not a writable field or `dry_run` (`v`, `id` and the other answer fields included), and `clear` any input key other than `dry_run`, as `sign_link.create` refuses a key outside its allow-list (`K/obop-commands/src/sign/link.rs:67-80`); the keys the dispatcher adds itself (`_caller` and the actor-prefixed ones) pass. A field whose value is not text is refused too.
- A refusal repeats no text of the value it refuses except what is made safe. It names a character by its code point (`U+200B`, and the character as well when it is printable ASCII), and an element, an attribute or a number of an artwork by the text that the reader makes safe to repeat (letters, digits and `: _ . - # %`, any other character shown as `?`, cut short). A refusal is not HTML: a page, a JSON string or a command line that shows one escapes it.
- `--title` and `--message` are global options of the binary today, one value each, for `status incident` (`crates/obop/src/main.rs:331,335`; its help is at `main.rs:139-143`), and `--dry-run` is a global flag (`main.rs:349-364`). `obop admin brand` takes its repeatable `--title LANG=TEXT` and `--message LANG=TEXT` before that handling, and a test keeps `status incident create --title T` working. The words `admin brand` are recognised at the start of the command line, after any global options and their values (`obop --db URL admin brand configure ...`); a `--title` or `--message` that is given before the words and so reaches the global handling of an `admin brand` command is refused as a usage error, so that no field is dropped without a word.

**The answers are declared fields.** A key of an answer that is not a declared field of the model is withheld on every surface (`K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:1287-1300`), so the keys of the three answers are declared on the model (a `CommandDef` has no output declaration of its own, `K/obop-registry/src/lib.rs:268-284`), as `sso_provider` declares its output-only fields `secret_set`, `break_glass` and `break_glass_enough` and its input `client_secret` (`K/obop-pkg/src/sso.rs:42-54`), and as `sign_link` declares `link`, which only the answer to `create` carries (`packages/sign.toml:258-262`). Their class is `none`, their group `*` (`ANY_GROUP`, `K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:55`). The registry has no object or list kind (`K/obop-registry/src/lib.rs:45-81`), so a structured part is a text field that holds JSON; its shape is stated here, not in a schema.

| Declared field | Kind | Where | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dry_run` | bool | input of `configure` and `clear`, echoed in the answer when true | Nothing was written |
| `set` | bool | `get` | A row exists |
| `v` | text | `get`, `configure` | The version of the logo; empty when no brand is set. In the answer of `configure` it is the version of the row after the change, computed with the theme setting as `configure` reads it (one more read before the write, so that a change that committed is never reported as a failure); the reader's cell keeps the accent it already holds, so with a generated tile this `v` and the one served can differ for up to two seconds after a theme change |
| `effective` | text, JSON object | `get` | For each public value of the table below: `{"value": ..., "source": ...}` |
| `fallbacks` | text, JSON list | `get` | Each stored field that failed the read check, in the order of the fields: `{"field": ..., "reason": ...}`. The reason is a fixed word (`empty`, `too_long`, `unsafe_character`, `invalid_slug`, or `not_text` for a stored value that is not text at all); for the artwork it is `<code>: <message>` of the first refusal of the SVG reader, with ` (and N more)` when there are more, or `not_text` when the stored artwork is not text |
| `changes` | text, JSON list | `configure` | `{"field": ..., "old": ..., "new": ...}` for each changed field; the artwork as old and new `v` (empty when there is none, `unreadable` for a stored artwork that the reader refuses); a text field whole, as stored |
| `refusals` | text, JSON list | `configure` with `dry_run` | `{"field": ..., "rule": ...}`. Without `dry_run` a refusal is the error; a handler's refusal is only a text (`CallError::Handler(String)`, `K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:164`), so its message lists the refusals, one per line as `field: rule`, and the typed list exists on `dry_run` only. An artwork adds at most 20 of them (section 8) |
| `cleared` | bool | `clear` | A row was removed (with `dry_run`: would be) |

The bool inputs (`dry_run`, `hide_product_docs`) accept the JSON booleans and also the texts `true` and `false`: `obop call` types an input from `/api/meta` (`crates/obop/src/main.rs:1067-1074`), this model is not in it, and so it sends text. `get` also returns the stored fields as stored, except `logo_svg`; the artwork is served by the logo route. A stored text that fails the safety subset of section 4 comes back in ASCII, every other character written as `\u{XXXX}` and the backslash doubled, because `obop call` prints the answer to a terminal and a value written around the command must not move a cursor or reorder a line; a stored value that is not text comes back as the empty text and is listed under `fallbacks`. MCP and the REST body schema give a hand-registered command the generic object schema (`K/obop-mcp/src/schema.rs:368-373`), so the input keys are named in the description of each command in the catalogue (`/api/commands`, the MCP tool text), as `sso_provider.configure` does (`K/obop-pkg/src/sso.rs:22-27`). What exists for the parity of D80 is therefore: the same command names, the same input keys and the same output field names and kinds on the three surfaces, held by a test (section 13). A typed input schema does not exist for any hand-registered command and is not added here.

**What `get` shows.** The effective brand, so that an operator can see why a surface still says obop. Each public value comes with its `source`, one of `record`, `env`, `site` (a `site.*` key), `theme` or `default`, and, for `authenticator_issuer` alone (S1e), `unreadable` with the value `null` where an enrolment is refused:

| Value | Possible sources |
|---|---|
| `name`, `slug`, `logo` (artwork or tile; the value of `logo` is `artwork`, `tile`, or `none` when no brand is set, never the artwork itself) | `record`, `default` |
| `title_<lang>`, `message_<lang>` (the value after the fallback chain of section 2) | `record`, `default` |
| `authenticator_issuer` (the label and `issuer=` of a new authenticator entry) | `env` (`OBOP_MFA_ISSUER`), `record`, `default`, and `unreadable` with the value `null` (the stored name failed the read check or cannot go into a label, so an enrolment is refused and there is no issuer in force; S1e) |
| `accent` (the sign-in screens: the theme's stored accent, else the stock violet) | `theme`, `default` |
| `tile_accent` (the generated tile and the shop's tile fallback, section 2) | `theme`, `default` (the slate) |
| `site.title`, `site.logo_url`, `site.accent`, favicon (the public site) | `site`, `record`, `theme`, `default` |

For the row of the public site above (slice S1f): the value of `favicon` is `artwork` when the shop's tab icon is the record's artwork and `tile` when it is the shop's own tile. The source of the accent is `site` for an explicit `site.accent`, else `theme` for the accent of the theme or `default` for the slate or teal; the source of the tab icon is `record` for the artwork, else `default`. The source of the title and of the logo route is `default`, not `record`, when the name of the row failed the read check and fell back to `obop` (the logo route only when the logo is the generated tile), because such a value is derived from the default name and from no stored field. A name of nothing but spaces fails the read check as `empty` and so is the default name (a title or a message of nothing but spaces fails it as `empty` too and is unset), and a name with a space at its start or end passes it: the title of the shop is then the name as the shop trims it, in `get` as on the page. With no record an explicit `site.*` key that the shop uses still has the source `site`, and (S1e) a value of `OBOP_MFA_ISSUER` still gives `authenticator_issuer` the source `env`, so `get` with no record is `set: false` with the defaults except for those entries, and `obop admin brand status` then heads its output "no record; every value is the default, except what a site.* key sets", "... except what OBOP_MFA_ISSUER sets" or, with both in force, "... except what a site.* key or OBOP_MFA_ISSUER sets" (at a terminal the variable is the one of the terminal's own process, section 3). A theme alone changes no header.

`effective` always lists the values of the record itself and the tile accent: `name`, `slug`, `logo`, `tile_accent`, `title_<lang>` and `message_<lang>`, each with its source. An entry whose precedence involves another source (the environment, a `site.*` key) or a surface that is not built is added by the slice that wires that surface, because before it the entry would say something untrue: `authenticator_issuer` arrives with the authenticator entry, the `site.*` entries and the favicon with the public site, and `accent` with the back office page. `fallbacks` lists the fields of a stored row that failed the read check and so fell back to the default, with the reason. With no row it returns the defaults with source `default` and `set: false`, which is an answer and not an error. A store that cannot be read is an error (the D74 503), not `set: false` with the defaults: `get` reads the row and the theme setting itself, through the reader's uncached read that returns the failure, never through `current()`, so a `get` right after a `configure` made in another process shows the new values. That is two small reads per call, and the Identity tab and the signed-in app call it once each. At a terminal `status` reports the environment of the terminal's own process, which may not be the server's; REST and MCP report the server's.

**Who.** Group `platform.admin` (`K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:52`) and a person. There is **one mechanism**: a `Dispatcher::around` wrapper on `instance_brand.configure` and `instance_brand.clear` that refuses unless `Principal::is_person()` (`K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:101`: a user principal with no scopes). It is the check that `sign_link.create` makes inside its handler (`K/obop-commands/src/sign/link.rs:52`), put in a wrapper as `sign_link.delete` and `sign_link.bulk_create` are wrapped (`link.rs:30-47`). That is the only check that also stops a mobile app's token, because that token keeps the owner's plain id with `scopes` set (`K/obop-auth/src/lib.rs:432-447`) and so an id test such as `person_only` of `sso_provider`, which looks for a `/agent` suffix (`K/obop-pkg/src/sso.rs:102-107`), would let it through. The wrapper runs when the command really runs, after the guards and after any approval, and an approved plan runs as the principal that proposed it (`K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:1094-1098`), so an agent's approved proposal is refused too; the commands declare no approval, so an agent gets a refusal outright, not a proposal (a deviation from D80, section 15.1). `sso_provider` is cited only for the D41 rule that a command is on every surface or on none (`K/obop-registry/src/lib.rs:949`), so `configure` and `clear` are listed in the MCP tools of a principal that holds `platform.admin` and refuse when they run. `get` has no wrapper: reading is open to any principal, an agent token included. On REST a person who does not hold `platform.admin` gets the answer of a command that does not exist, the 404 `{"error":"not found"}`, as for every command a caller may not run, because the dispatcher answers a missing group, an unknown command and a command that is not on the surface alike (`K/obop-commands/src/errors.rs:581-590`); an agent, a scoped token or a mobile token that does hold the group gets the refusal of the wrapper, a 400 whose message says that the branding is changed by a person. The reason for the guard is that a name and a logo decide what a person believes they are signing in to.

**Only these commands exist.** A command exists on the REST, MCP and CLI surfaces only where a handler is bound (`Dispatcher::register` fills the command table, `K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:679-760`), so the model is hand-registered like `sso_provider`: `get`, `configure` and `clear` and nothing else. The generated `create`, `update`, `delete`, `bulk_create` (and with it `obop import`), `list` and `search` do not exist for it; `register_crud` is not called for it (`K/obop-commands/src/store.rs:299-345`). Were a later change to bind them, each would have to be refused through the same `around` wrapper, as `sign_link` refuses `bulk_create` (`K/obop-commands/src/sign/link.rs:42-47`). A test asserts the commands are absent or refused (section 13). Because the model has no `list`, `/api/meta` and the authenticated OpenAPI document do not list it (a model appears there only with a visible `list`, `K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:543`). No action effect reaches the row either. An effect of a package writes a record the way the generated `create` and `update` do, so it reaches only a model that has them, and a model whose commands are hand-registered (this one, `sso_provider`, the settings, the logs) is changed by its own commands alone, with their guards and their audit. The install gate refuses a package whose action effect names such a model that the package does not declare, and the engine refuses the same write when an effect runs, also for an action that an effect starts with `set_state` (every depth beyond the action the caller named), so a package stored before the gate is held too. The rule is general: it holds for every hand-registered model and every package. `obop modules check FILE` does not run the gate; the installer and the engine do. A package cannot give one of its models the name of a built-in model (`instance_brand` and `security_event` among them) or of a table that the program keeps for itself, because its generated `create`, `update` and `delete` would reach the row or the log around the guards and the audit of the commands of that model, and it cannot take the id of a module that the program registered itself, because the module's commands would be removed with the package's at its next uninstall, upgrade or sync. An id is the program's when it is that of a built-in module, of a module with a command that the binary registers before the installer is made (such as the one for the SIE exchange), or of any module with a command that something other than the installer has registered by the time the package is registered. The gaps are listed in docs/ISSUES.md as pull request 69 wrote them: N17 (a scan of the code holds the list of tables and does not see a name built at run time; a recorder of the real application sees only the tables its flows ask for, 12 of the 55; the models of bundled packages that the program also names, such as the shop's orders, are not reserved until their package is installed), K57 (a program module registered after a package of the same id) and K58 (a program module that has only an observer or a guard). Installing a package needs `platform.admin`, and K57 and K58 are not reachable in the binary today. The install and upgrade plan refuses the name (`model X already exists`) and the id (a blocker that names the module), and the one function that registers a package refuses both for every route that goes through it, install, upgrade, start-up, the sync with another process and rollback, so a package stored before the name or the id was taken, or one that another process stored, is not registered and its records are left as they are. The installer unregisters only the modules it registered itself, so a stored record that carries the id of a module of the program does not stand for that module: it can be uninstalled, which removes the record and leaves the module as it is. A stored record whose key differs from the id of its manifest is not registered and is not listed or counted as an installed package, and a rollback to a manifest of another id is refused.

**At a terminal** `obop admin brand` acts on the database as the operator, as `obop site set` does (`crates/obop/src/main.rs:1343-1363`) and as `obop admin sso configure` does through `operator_sso_configure` (`crates/obop/src/accounts_cmd.rs:218-220`): a person with the host, not a token. It calls the same `brand::apply`, with the actor `operator`, and so takes the same lock. It opens the database without the demo data that other commands seed into a new SQLite file, because the first thing an operator does to a new instance is `configure` (section 14), and the brand must not be the first write to a database that then holds a demo company. The exit status is 0 when the command did what it was asked; 2 for a command line that is spelled wrong (an unknown word or flag, a flag with no value, a field given twice, `--clear-logo` with `--logo-file`, nothing to set), as for the usage text of any command; 1 for a value, a file or a store that was refused or could not be read; 75 when the store refuses the call with a D74 code word (`storage_busy`, `storage_unavailable`, `storage_timeout`), with the line that `obop call` prints for it. A store that cannot be read is never "no brand". A `configure --dry-run` that finds refusals prints each as `error: field: rule`, says that a real call would be refused and exits 1, so that a script can use the dry run as a check; on REST and MCP the same dry run is a normal answer with the typed `refusals`. `brand::apply` takes the Reader as an optional argument: the dispatcher passes its own and the CLI passes none, so a change made at a terminal reaches a running server within two seconds, as a change made by another process does. The Reader's cell and counter (section 4) are updated after the atomic batch has committed and never before, so a batch that fails to commit leaves the cell as it was. The back office tab (section 6) calls the three commands and nothing else, so there is no UI-only path.

**Audit.** `brand::apply` writes the row and the events in **one atomic batch** (`Backend::apply_checked`: `Op::Put` of the row, or `Op::Delete` for `clear`, and one `audit::event_op` per changed field, `K/obop-commands/src/audit.rs:74`, `K/obop-storage/src/lib.rs:38`). The entry then exists exactly when the change does. This is not the settings precedent, which writes the data first and the event afterwards and only prints a failure to stderr (`K/obop-pkg/src/installer.rs:1437-1451`, `K/obop-commands/src/audit.rs:47-58`). Each event has kind `brand_configured` or `brand_cleared`, the actor, and a detail of one line that names the field and holds the **old and the new value** of that field, `field: old -> new` (for `clear`, `field: old -> removed`). A text is the JSON text of the value, `""` when unset (the first `configure --name MOHV` logs `name: "" -> "MOHV"`); the artwork is `v=<16 hex digits>`, `none` when there is none and `unreadable` when the stored artwork fails the reader. The `user` argument of `event_op` is `(instance)`, as `set_settings_as` writes it (`K/obop-pkg/src/installer.rs:1446`), and the actor is the principal's id, or `operator` at a terminal. `clear` writes one `brand_cleared` event for every key the row holds, in the order of the fields and then the other keys in the order of their names, so that the log accounts for all that the delete removes: a text field of the record with its old value (an empty or null one as `""`, the artwork as above), a field of the record whose stored value is not text as `field: <not text> -> removed`, and a key that this binary does not know (a newer binary's field) as `key: <unknown> -> removed`, the last two with no value, because what such a value is cannot be written safely into a line of the log. A key is written as it is shown in a refusal (ASCII letters, digits, `_` and `-`, any other character as `?`, cut at 40 characters with three dots), so two keys that differ only in such characters read alike. At most 50 unknown keys are written one by one; the rest are one event, `and N more keys: <unknown> -> removed` (`and 1 more key: <unknown> -> removed` for one), so that a row written around the command cannot make the batch, and the lock it holds, as long as it likes. A row with no key at all is removed with no event, and with no row nothing is removed and nothing is written. `hide_product_docs` is such an unknown key until S2 adds it to the fields that `clear` logs. All fields are class `none` and at most 240 characters when `configure` writes them, so the values are written as they are; the artwork is recorded as the old and new `v`, since 16 KiB does not belong in a log line. A value written around the command that does not pass the safety subset of section 4 (a control, format, zero-width or bidirectional character, a space other than U+0020, too long; section 4 has the list) is written in quotes with printable ASCII and the space as they are, every other character (an ASCII control included) as `\u{XXXX}` with at least four hex digits, the backslash doubled and the double quote escaped, and cut at 300 characters, then three dots after the closing quote, so that a stored value cannot forge or reorder a line of the log; the answer `changes` carries such a value whole. After a `clear` or an overwrite the log therefore shows what users saw, with the artwork as its version and a long or unsafe value cut or escaped. Both kinds are added to the list in the description of `security_event.kind` (`K/obop-pkg/src/installer.rs:2442-2445`), which `K/obop-http/src/audit_kind_tests.rs` holds to the code.

## 4. Where it is stored, and how each surface reads it

**Record, not environment.** The existing branding-like settings are environment variables read once at start (`OBOP_MFA_ISSUER`, `K/obop-auth/src/mfa.rs:99-101`; `OBOP_DEFAULT_LANGUAGE`, `K/obop-http/src/shell.rs:1-9`). That does not fit here: (1) an environment variable is not a command, and every surface must have one; (2) a logo of 16 KiB does not belong in an environment variable; (3) several server processes on one PostgreSQL database must agree without a restart, which a record read through a short cache gives and an environment variable cannot; (4) a change must be audited and the environment has no author. Precedent for a record read at request time exists: `Auth::security_setting` reads `obop_setting/instance` each time (`K/obop-auth/src/mfa.rs:147-151`) and `site_settings` does the same for the public side (`K/obop-commands/src/public.rs:745-757`). No new environment variable is added in S1, and `OBOP_MFA_ISSUER` and `OBOP_DEFAULT_LANGUAGE` keep working (section 5).

**Not inside `obop_setting/instance`.** That row is one JSON text that `setting.update` replaces whole for any principal that holds `platform.admin`, with no person-only check (`K/obop-pkg/src/installer.rs:2614-2626`), and its one field is class `none` with no per-field rule. The brand needs per-field validation and a person-only guard, so it is its own model. The theme stays where it is. That leaves a stated limit: `setting.update` can still change the theme accent (which joins the public subset and sets the tile fill) and the `site.*` keys for any principal that holds `platform.admin`, an agent token included, and it writes no security event for either: `set_settings_as` logs only the changes under `security` (`K/obop-pkg/src/installer.rs:1426-1451`, `security_changes` at `installer.rs:1998-2023`). This record does not change that (section 14).

**One reader.** `Brand` and its rules live in a new leaf crate `obop-brand` (the validation and character policy, the slug, the SVG reader, the tile with the colour maths it needs, the neutral-wording switch; no I/O, as `obop-values` is a leaf crate; it depends on neither `obop-commands` nor `obop-site`). The reader, `obop_commands::brand::Reader`, is held by the `Dispatcher`, which already holds the services of other packages the same way (`blob_service` and `sign_service`, `K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:285,288`): `register_admin` creates it once as an `OnceLock<Arc<Reader>>`, because it has the store and runs in every composition (`crates/obop/src/app.rs:88`), and `HttpState` and `PublicApi` read it through `dispatcher.brand()`, so no constructor signature changes; a `Dispatcher` that never ran `register_admin` (some test rigs) gives the default brand. `Auth` needs only the uncached read and calls the free function `brand::strict(&dyn Backend)`. There is no global. The consumers, as far as reading the routes and files cited here finds them: the shell (S2), the OAuth handlers, the signer door, the connector return page, `Auth` (the authenticator issuer), the two passkey context builders, the logo route (below), the handler of `instance_brand.get`, the public site and shop and, from S3, the MCP server (`initialize`, the server card, the discovery documents), the OpenAPI builder and the Slack message prefix. The public site and shop reach the Reader through `PublicApi::site_settings_read` (the call the site makes; `site_settings` is the same call answering the object alone, and is the one at `K/obop-commands/src/public.rs:745-757` of the base), the only call that feeds the site's `Settings` (`K/obop-site/src/lib.rs:137`): it fills the fallbacks of section 5 into the map it returns and hands the artwork through one reserved key of that map, whose value is an object `{svg, v}` that the Reader computed (the shop hashes and re-derives nothing, and its `ETag` is the logo route's, `"<v>"`, the quoted `v`; the code names the key with a constant that `obop-commands` defines and `obop-site` imports). `setting.update` can write any key of the `site` object (`K/obop-pkg/src/installer.rs:2614-2626`), so `site_settings` first strips that key from the stored object, and an administrator cannot put an artwork on the shop's `/favicon.svg` or `/favicon.ico` through the `site.*` keys (a test, section 13). `obop-site` never names the model. No code outside the module `obop_commands::brand` reads the model (a scan, section 13); inside it four things do: the Reader's refill, the free function `strict()`, `apply` under its lock, and the uncached read that `get` (section 3) makes for itself, `Reader::fresh()`, which two callers outside the command also take: the command line `obop admin brand status` and, from S3, the signer of a Slack message (a message that has left cannot be taken back, so it does not take the default that `current()` gives behind a failed first refill, docs/ISSUES.md PS3-9). The module gives its callers three reads, the cached `current()`, the uncached `fresh()` and the uncached `strict()`.

- `current()` returns the brand for display as one immutable snapshot: the `Brand`, its logo with `v`, the accent that the theme stores and the `fallbacks`, so that a page takes the logo and `v` from the same read as the name. On every refill each field is checked by the rule of its class. The *text fields* (`name`, `title_<lang>`, `message_<lang>`) are checked against a *safety subset* of the policy, not the whole of it: the length limit, and none of a control, format, private-use or surrogate code point, a default-ignorable one, a space other than U+0020, or `<` and `>` (the output is escaped anyway); `slug` also its fixed pattern (`a-z`, `0-9` and `-`, starting with a letter, not ending with `-`, at most 30), because the slug reaches `serverInfo.name`, the meta tag and a file name. Unassigned code points are not in the read-side subset: whether a code point is assigned depends on the Unicode tables of the binary, and a name stored by a newer binary, or restored from its backup, must not fall back on an older one; the write-side policy of section 2 refuses them. That subset applies to the text fields only. *The artwork* (`logo_svg`) holds `<` and `>` in every valid file and is inlined into pages without escaping, so it has its own check: the Reader parses the stored text with the SVG reader of section 8. If the parse fails (a script element, a link, an attribute outside the list, a number outside the rule, more than 16 KiB, a byte order mark) the tile is used instead. If it succeeds, the Reader serves, inlines and hashes the printer's output for that parse, never the stored text, and computes `v` from those bytes. There is no comparison with the stored text: a stored artwork that is merely not in canonical form (the output of an older writer, or a row written around the command) is valid and is served re-serialised, and a later release that changes the writer changes the bytes served and `v`, not whether a stored logo is valid. The printer is idempotent (printing its own output gives the same bytes; a property test, section 13), but nothing in the read check depends on that. `hide_product_docs` is read as a boolean only; any other stored value reads as false. The rest of the policy (scripts, the refused forms of section 2) is applied when `configure` writes the value, so a policy tightened in a later release never reverts a stored name. A field that fails its check (a row written by a restore, an import, a direct edit or an older binary) falls back to its default for that field only (`name` to `obop`, `slug` to the slug derived from the effective name, a title or message to empty, the artwork to the tile, the switch to off) and is never rendered raw; a refill writes one line to stderr, once for each field for the life of the reader (the dispatcher holds one, so once per server process; the key is the field, not the reason), that names the field and a fixed reason word of the code and never the value (so a stored value cannot shape the line), and points to `obop admin brand status`; `strict()` and `get` write none, and `get` lists the field under `fallbacks`. A stored value that is not text at all (a number, a list) fails its check with the reason word `not_text`. The refill is one unit, the row and the theme setting together: if either read fails, the refill fails, and the paragraph on the display read and D74, below, says what `current()` then returns.
S1c amends the safety subset of the text fields: a text of nothing but U+0020 counts as empty, so a name falls back to `obop` and a title to the name instead of showing a blank line (the write check refuses such a text, so it can only come from a row written around the command).
- *Spaces only (S1f).* S1c made the rule above (a text of nothing but U+0020 is empty on the read side), and S1f is where the shop applies it, for each of the three text fields, because a page could show none of such a text (a blank line beside the logo, an empty paragraph under the lead). A `name` of nothing but spaces fails the safety subset as `empty` and is the default name; a `title_<lang>` or `message_<lang>` of nothing but spaces fails it as `empty` too, so it is unset: the fallback chain goes on to the English text and then to the name (or to no message), and `get` lists it under `fallbacks`. An absent title or message (the empty text) is unset and is no failure, and a space at the start or the end of a text passes (it is no danger on a page). `configure` never lets such a text in; only a row written around the command holds one.
- `strict()` is used only where the text is written into the person's own device and cannot be corrected afterwards: the TOTP enrolment `begin` (when `OBOP_MFA_ISSUER` is not set; the environment value wins and needs no read) and the passkey *registration* options (`rp.name`, `K/obop-auth/src/passkey.rs:619`, emitted with `residentKey: preferred`, `passkey.rs:626`). Sign-in, the passkey authentication options (the sign-in ceremony emits no name, `passkey.rs:445-471`) and every other passkey route do not use it. It never answers from the cache: it reads the row itself, every time, and not the theme, so an enrolment that begins a moment after a `configure` made in another process sees the new name, and a stale or failed answer is never written to a device. It fails when the store read fails, when the stored `name` fails the safety subset, or (S1e) when the name passes the safety subset and still cannot go into a label: a `:` `/` `\` or `@` (which `configure` refuses as `link_character`; an app splits the decoded label at its colon and would show another issuer than `issuer=` says). A name of nothing but U+0020 does not get that far: since S1c the safety subset calls it empty, so it is the first case, and a name of any other white space is refused by the safety subset as it always was; the label check keeps an arm for a name of white space only (a second line of defence that no stored name reaches since S1c). No row at all is not a failure; the default is the answer. A failure is a 503 on both enrolments and nothing is written. An enrolment is authenticated and rare, so the extra read costs nothing.

Two causes, two answers. *The store read fails:* `strict()` returns the storage error text unchanged, so a transient fault (its text starts with a D74 code word, `storage_busy`, `storage_unavailable` or `storage_timeout`) takes the existing transient arm of `mfa_error` (`K/obop-http/src/mfa.rs`) and is answered as D74 requires, 503 with `Retry-After` (`unavailable` in `K/obop-http/src/lib.rs`). *Anything else* (a read error that is not a transient fault, or a stored `name` that fails the safety subset) is `MfaError::Unavailable` with a fixed message that starts "the instance branding could not be read". The generic arm of `mfa_error` would give such a message the hint "Ask the operator to set OBOP_SECRET_KEY", the wrong cause, so S1 adds an arm after the transient arm and before that one, matching the prefix, and answers 503 with the code `brand_unavailable` and a hint that names the branding (`obop admin brand status`). It sets no `Retry-After`, since waiting does not repair a stored name. Because the new arm comes after the transient one, a transient fault never reaches it; the tests assert each case (section 13). The REST passkey builder `ctx()` (`K/obop-http/src/passkeys.rs`) serves every passkey route, sign-in included, and fills `rp_name` from `current()` (the base carried the constant `obop` there and read no store, so since S1e a sign-in makes a display read it did not make before; "The display read and D74" below and section 14 say what follows); only the `register_options` handler calls `strict()`, and as built in S1e it builds its site with the name of `strict()` alone, after the person is authenticated and with no display read, so the theme setting cannot refuse it. A bad stored name therefore does not stop passkey or TOTP sign-in, and an administrator who is already enrolled and signed in can still open the Identity tab and repair it. An administrator who must still enrol (D53) holds a limited session that allows nothing, not even the any-principal group (`K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:108-111`), so for that person the enrolment is refused and the Identity tab is closed; the repair is `obop admin brand configure` or `obop admin brand clear` at the terminal, and the `docs/DEPLOY.md` section for S1 says so.

**The display read and D74.** A failed refill is not kept, and it does not make a page say `obop`. When a refill fails, `current()` returns the last good snapshot this process held, of any age, and the default only in a process that never had a good read (a caller that has no last good value waits for the running refill, below, and shares its outcome); the next caller after the failure starts a refill of its own. The request that ran the failed read is another matter. The pool noted the fault on its thread, and `ops::storage_faults` answers every request that met a storage fault with the D74 503, unless a change was committed and the request ends in a success, which keeps its own answer (`K/obop-http/src/ops.rs:255-302`); `obop_storage::fault::before_write` then refuses that request's later writes (`K/obop-storage/src/fault.rs:259`). The display read is not run inside `obop_storage::fault::detached` (`fault.rs:402`) to hide this. For the OAuth and signer pages that is right: a request that cannot read the store cannot do what it came for either, since the session, the client and the flow it needs are in the same store, and D74 has already decided the answer. For the back-office index document it is a choice, not a necessity: the index could be served from the file without the store, and the sign-in it leads to cannot; one rule (D74 decides) is kept in place of an exception for the shell. The same holds for the passkey routes, whose `ctx()` calls `current()` also where no name is needed (list, rename, remove). `fault::detached` around the display refill of the shell and of `ctx()`, serving the last good brand, is the named alternative; the choice is among the changes made after the card (15.3 (f)). So that a write is not refused only because the brand was asked for late, a handler asks the Reader once, first, and uses that one `Brand` for every page it renders. `strict()` and `get` do not hide a failure at all: they return it as an error. Tests pin each case with the fault-injection doubles (section 13).

`Brand::default()` is name `obop`, no artwork, no titles, no messages, neutral wording off, and so is any instance with no row; every consumer asks `Brand` for the name and so takes one code path with or without a row. A golden corpus of names that `configure` must keep accepting is part of the tests (section 13), so a release that tightens the character policy fails CI instead of refusing a customer's own name on its next save.

**The cache.** The reader keeps the outcome of its last good refill (the parsed `Brand` from the row, the accent read from `obop_setting/instance`, the logo bytes and `v`) in the process for at most two seconds, counted from the start of the read that filled it and not from its end, in a cell behind a standard mutex. That mutex is held for a moment and never across a call of the store, and a poisoned one is recovered with `into_inner`, as `/readyz` recovers its own (`K/obop-http/src/ops.rs:379,396`); the shop's cache takes its own mutex with a bare `unwrap` around the call that reaches the Reader (`K/obop-site/src/lib.rs:131`), so a panic in the Reader would poison the shop's page cache until restart, which is why the Reader, the policy checks and the SVG reader are total functions: whatever text a row holds, they return a value or a refusal and never panic (tests, section 13). The cache-hit path takes the cell's mutex, hands out the snapshot (a shared pointer, not a copy of the brand) and never touches the store. The refill is single-flight, and a caller that has a last good value never parks on it: a second mutex guards the refill, and a caller whose cell is stale (empty, or older than two seconds) takes it with `try_lock`. There are three results. *Obtained:* this caller owns the refill. It first looks at the cell again, because a caller that read the stale time just before the previous flight stored its outcome gets the lock right after that flight, and finds the cell fresh; it returns that value and reads nothing, so one expiry costs one read and not two. If the cell is still stale it reads the row and the theme setting and stores the outcome. *Poisoned* (an earlier owner panicked while it held the lock): the lock is obtained all the same, and recovered with `into_inner` like the cell's mutex; it is never taken for "a refill is running", because then the last good brand would be served for ever and no refill would run again. *Would block:* a refill is running. A caller that has a last good value serves it at once and does not wait. A caller that has none waits for that refill: it blocks on the refill mutex (a poisoned lock is recovered with `into_inner` like the others), and when it gets the lock it reads the outcome that the owner left in a slot of the refill, a good value or the failure, and answers with it (the default for a failure) when the slot is the outcome of that flight. It refills for itself, as an owner (looking at the cell again first), in the three cases where the slot is not an answer for it: the slot is empty (the owner panicked); a `configure` or `clear` of this process committed while the flight ran (the outcome may be the row that was replaced); or the slot is of a flight that was over before the caller found the cell stale (the lock was held by another waiter that was reading the slot, not by a running flight). The slot belongs to one flight and is not the cache: the next flight replaces it, and a caller that arrives later and finds the cell stale never reads it. Only a process that has never had a good read has such a caller, and the first caller of that process is the owner of the refill itself, so the request that waits for the store is that one, and its answer is the D74 503 when the read fails (below); a caller that arrives behind it is the one that waits for the flight. The wait is as long as the flight. The flight is two point reads (`Backend::get`, `K/obop-storage/src/pg.rs:402-403`), the row and the theme setting, each bounded on PostgreSQL by the pool's acquire timeout, 2 seconds by default (`K/obop-storage/src/pool.rs:67`, `K/obop-storage/src/pgpool.rs:90`), and by the time limit of a point statement, 5 seconds by default (`pgpool.rs:97`; D74, `pgpool.rs:3`). No extra timeout of the kind `/readyz` puts around its call (`READY_TIMEOUT`, `ops.rs:36`) is added: with this rule one thread per process is inside the store for the brand, the other callers hold the cell's mutex for a moment, and those limits are the bound. The outcome of a good refill is kept for the two seconds; a failed one is not kept (above), so that the first call after the store recovers reads again and nothing stale or default is pinned. The price is stated: during an outage the cache is effectively off, each flight costs one failed read and the flights follow one another (a refusal by a full pool queue comes at once), which is what any other read of the store costs a request then. `/readyz` does keep a failed answer for two seconds (`ops.rs:377-397`); the brand does not. `strict()` neither reads the theme nor uses this cache. Two seconds is the bound that D67 sets for the one unauthenticated route that reads the database (`/readyz` "costs the database one ping and one read per two seconds however hard it is hit", `docs/DECISIONS.md:763`). The shell is the fallback for every unmatched path (`spa_with_json_api_404` in `K/obop-http/src/lib.rs`), so scanner traffic for `/wp-login.php` or `/.env` would otherwise cost a read each. A `configure` or `clear` through this process puts the brand it just wrote into its own cell at once and drops nothing: once the atomic batch has committed, `brand::apply` builds the snapshot of the row it wrote (after a `clear`, the default) and replaces the brand part of the cell (the `Brand`, the logo bytes and `v`), with no extra read of the store. It takes the cell's mutex twice, a moment each time: first to bump the counter (below) and to take the accent and the age of the value, then to put the new value in. The parse and the hash of the artwork happen between, with no lock held, and the value is replaced only if the age of the cell is unchanged (a refill that began after the bump and stored meanwhile read the new row, and its value stays). The cell keeps its theme part and its age. So a process that has read once has no moment without a last good value, a refill that fails right after a change serves the brand that was just written and never the one it replaced or cleared, and the change shows at once in this process; a process that has not read yet has nothing to replace, and its first read reads the new row. `apply` also bumps a counter of the Reader under that mutex, and a refill stores its outcome only if the counter is the one it started under: a refill that read the old row before the change and finishes after it gives its outcome to its own caller and keeps nothing, so it cannot put the replaced brand back. A theme change (`setting.update`) and a change made through another process show within two seconds. The public site and shop add a layer of their own in front of the Reader: the site keeps its parsed settings for 5 seconds by default (`K/obop-site/src/lib.rs:48,56,130-137`, `docs/STOREFRONT.md` section "Settings") and asks `PublicApi::site_settings_read`, hence the Reader, only on a refresh, so the shop's server may answer with the old name for up to those 5 seconds plus the Reader's 2 (a browser or a shared cache in front of the shop may keep a page 60 seconds more and a stale copy 300 more, because the shop sends its pages with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=60, stale-while-revalidate=300`), and `configure` does not drop the site's cache. D59 (BINDING, `docs/DECISIONS.md:657`) says nothing that decides an answer lives only in one process's memory, and lets an in-memory cache follow a shared version number; this cache is a conscious deviation (15.1): a bounded copy of a database value, which the database corrects within the bound and a restart refills. A shared version number would not cover a theme change made by `setting.update`, which does not touch the brand row but changes the accent that the tile and the meta tag carry, so the time bound is used, and it covers both. While the store answers, the cost is at most one read of the row and one of the theme setting per process per two seconds for every caller of `current()`, however many requests arrive, apart from one more read when a configure or clear of this process lands while a refill runs (section 9); `strict()` adds one read of the row per enrolment and `get` its own reads per call. Tests build the reader with a zero bound.

**Pages before sign-in.** The back office shows the sign-in, second-factor, enrolment and forced-password screens before the settings can be read, and no pre-sign-in route carries a setting today: "no other setting is public" (`K/obop-http/src/shell.rs:3-8`; `/api/meta` and `/api/commands` are routes of `api_routes` in `K/obop-http/src/lib.rs` that answer 401 without a token, which is from the render check). The two places that build a `Shell` use the same reader: `spa_with_json_api_404` (`K/obop-http/src/lib.rs`) and the public-site router that nests it under `--admin-prefix` (`K/obop-http/src/public.rs:86`). **The logo route.** `GET /api/brand/logo.svg` (public, like `/api/openapi.json`, both routes of `api_routes` in `K/obop-http/src/lib.rs`) serves the artwork as the Reader re-serialised it (section 4) or the tile, and nothing else; it takes no path or id, so it is not a file server, and a query string is ignored except that it is part of the cache key of the browser. Headers: `Content-Type: image/svg+xml`, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; style-src 'none'; sandbox`, `Cache-Control: no-cache`, `ETag` = `"<v>"`, the quoted strong validator of `v` (an `If-None-Match` that matches, weakly, `*` or any entry of its list, answers 304 with the `ETag`, the `Cache-Control` and the two security headers, no `Content-Type` and no body. The handler sets the `Content-Length` of the 200 on that 304, the one value RFC 9110 section 8.6 allows there; the HTTP server sends it on a `HEAD` 304 and omits it from a `GET` 304, which the same section allows, so on the wire only a `HEAD` 304 carries it. A `HEAD` is the `GET` without the body). No brand set: for `GET` and `HEAD`, 404 with the answer that the layout gives a one-segment path of the API that no route owns (`/api/nothing`), status, headers and body byte for byte: the JSON with a code and a hint beside the API alone and beside the back office build (`api_not_found_response` in `K/obop-http/src/lib.rs`), the short `{"error":"not found"}` in the public site's router (`json_404`, `public.rs:150-156`). The handler is registered once in `api_routes` (`K/obop-http/src/lib.rs`), which every layout builds on (`K/obop-http/src/public.rs:84`), and each layout passes it its own answer to an unknown path; where in the list of routes it is registered does not matter (a literal route wins over `/api/{model}/{command}`, and no test pins the order). That is the whole of the property, and the code and its test say no more: the route does not hide that it exists. A path of the same shape with two segments, `/api/zzz/logo.svg`, is matched by `/api/{model}/{command}` and answers a `GET` with a 405 and `Allow: POST`; the logo path answers every method but `GET` and `HEAD` with a 405 and `Allow: GET,HEAD`. While the store cannot be read, only the request that runs the failing refill answers the 503 of D74 where an unknown path answers 404 (the display read and D74, above); while the cell is fresh, or a refill is in flight and a last good logo is held, the other requests get the last good logo, and a request that waits behind a failed first read in a process that has never had a good read gets the no-brand 404. A scanner can tell that the route exists. With no brand, a `GET` and a `HEAD` of the path answer exactly what a one-segment unknown API path answers in that layout; a set brand shows itself by the 200 and the artwork, which is public by design (section 2), and the route shows nothing else about the instance. The 404 is the layout's answer and so carries no `Cache-Control` (the 200 is `no-cache`): a shared cache in front of an instance may keep it by its own heuristics for a while after a brand is set, and the address with `?v=` is made only once a brand is set (section 9). Before S1b the path was no route's, in any layout: it matched the one route `/api/{model}/{command}` (model `brand`, command `logo.svg`), which `api_routes` registers for `POST` only, and the public-site layout builds on the same routes (`K/obop-http/src/public.rs:84`). The fallbacks (`api_not_found`, the back-office fallback, and `json_404` in `handle`, `public.rs:101-104`) run only for a path that no route matches, and a path whose route lacks the method does not reach them: axum answers it with its own 405 (axum 0.8.9, the one `Cargo.lock` pins, `docs/routing/fallback.md` and `MethodRouter::new`; the code never sets a `method_not_allowed_fallback`; read in the source, and then measured by the first test run of S1b). So before S1b a `GET` of the path answered 405 with an empty body and `Allow: POST`, not a 404, and a `POST` reached `call` (`call_blocking` in `K/obop-http/src/lib.rs`): 401 with no body without a bearer token (`AuthRejection::Unauthorized`), and with one the 404 `not_found` JSON of an unknown command (`K/obop-commands/src/errors.rs:581-590`, and `CallError::UnknownCommand` raised in `Dispatcher::call`, `K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs`). The existing 404 tests use a one-segment path that no route matches (`/api/nothing` at `crates/obop/src/public_site_tests.rs:155,369`, `/api/nothing-here` at `crates/obop/tests/startup_config.rs:500`) and say nothing of this path. S1b registers the logo route as a literal `GET` route, as `/api/blobs/{id}` is a get-only route beside the parameter route (both in `api_routes`), and that changes the answers of the path on purpose: a `GET` with no brand is the 404 above (with a brand, the 200 of this paragraph), a `POST` to the path answers 405, with `Allow: GET,HEAD` and no body, and no longer reaches `call` (read from axum and then measured by the first test run of slice S1b, which pinned it, section 13), and so does every other method (`PUT`, `PATCH`, `DELETE` and an `OPTIONS` that is no preflight answer 405 with `Allow: GET,HEAD` where it was `Allow: POST`), a `HEAD` is the answer of the route (the 200 with a brand, the 404 of the layout without) where it was a 405 with `Allow: POST`, and the `Allow` of a cross-origin preflight of the path is `GET,HEAD` where it was `POST`, as for the preflight of `/api/openapi.json`. These are deliberate changes of an existing answer, on a path that no route of its own served until now (sections 12 and 13): five of them, the `GET`, the `POST`, the other methods, the `HEAD` and the preflight. ## 8. The OAuth and signing pages: policy, logo, SVG, escaping

**Policy stays.** Both pages send `default-src 'none'; style-src 'sha256-<hash of CSS>'; …` and no `img-src` (`page.rs:151-165`, `K/obop-http/src/sign_page.rs:54-58`); the hash is computed from the fixed `CSS` constant, and the passkey script is allowed by its own hash. Rules this proposal keeps: no new script, no `style=` attribute, no new rule in `CSS` (a changed `CSS` changes the hash and every page's bytes), no `<img>`. So the logo on these two pages is inline `<svg>` with presentation attributes (`width`, `height`, `viewBox`, `fill`, `stroke`, `d`), which the policy does not govern. The stored artwork has no `width` or `height` (below), and an inline `<svg>` with only a `viewBox` grows to the width of its container, so the page template writes `width="28" height="28"` on the inline root: the pixel size is a constant of the template (28 by 28 is a proposal), not stored and not CSS, and it is not in the bytes the logo route serves or in `v`. The printer takes the size as a parameter, so the root is written once; the inline text differs from the served bytes by those two attributes. How the inline logo sits on the text baseline was not tried (section 14). The one addition is the icon link: when it is emitted the two policies gain `img-src 'self'`, because browsers differ in whether a tab icon is held to `img-src` and which do is not verified here. With no brand set neither the link nor the directive exists.
S1c: the template wraps the inline logo in `<span aria-hidden="true">` and puts a space between it and the name: the logo is decoration, since the name follows it, so a screen reader reads the name once. S1c tried how the inline logo sits on the text baseline on the OAuth pages (section 14, last bullet), and verified for one Chrome that a tab icon is held to `img-src` (the same bullet); Firefox and Safari are not verified.

**Serving the logo to an unauthenticated page.** There is no file serving. The artwork is one stored value, served by one fixed route (section 4) or inlined by the server into the page it renders. The server never serves an uploaded file as it came, never follows a URL in the artwork, and never takes a path or id.

**SVG rules.** Input is parsed by a strict, hand-written reader in `obop-brand`; the stored text is a re-serialisation from the parsed values, so a parser bug can refuse or mis-read a logo but cannot store hostile markup, and the read side serves the printer's output for its own parse of the stored text (section 4), so a row written around the command reaches a page only as the printer wrote it. The reader is a total function of its input: whatever the text (malformed, truncated, deeply nested, huge), it returns a refusal or an output and never panics, and its depth and size are bounded by the rules below (128 elements, nesting of 4, 16 KiB). The subset is wide enough for a logo exported from a design tool after it has been cleaned, because the owner's "swap the real logo later with one file" depends on it, and `check` and `dry_run` (section 3) say what to remove, 20 reasons at a time.

- Accepted: an optional `<?xml version … ?>` line (not stored); one root `<svg>` with a `viewBox` of four finite numbers whose width and height are positive and, rounded to 4 decimals, not zero, and with an optional `width` and `height`, which are ignored and not stored but must each be a plain number (by the rules for numbers below) with an optional unit (`px`, `pt`, `em`, `cm`, `mm`, `in` or `%`), so `width="auto"` is refused; at most 128 elements in all, of these kinds: `<g>` (grouping only, nested at most 4 deep), `<path d>`, `<rect x y width height rx>`, `<circle cx cy r>`, `<ellipse cx cy rx ry>`, `<line x1 y1 x2 y2>`, `<polygon points>`.
- Attributes per element: `fill` and `stroke` as `#rgb`, `#rrggbb`, `none` or `currentColor`; `fill-rule` as `nonzero` or `evenodd`; `stroke-width` as a finite non-negative number; `transform` as a list of at most four `matrix(a b c d e f)`, `translate(x y)` or `scale(x y)` with finite numbers. Every attribute value is a number list, a word of a closed set (`none`, `currentColor`, `nonzero`, `evenodd`) or a hex colour, and the writer prints it from the parsed value: no attribute value is ever copied from the input text, and any other value is refused with a named reason. A `transform` is re-printed from the parsed numbers; it is not folded. Rotate, skew and any other function are refused (a design tool can write a rotation as a `matrix`).
- Numbers. On input a number is a plain decimal of at most 24 characters and of absolute value at most 100000: an optional sign, then digits with an optional fraction, where either side of the point may be empty but not both (`5`, `5.5`, `.5` and `5.` are numbers; design tools and SVGO write `.5`), and no exponent; any other number refuses the file, and the refusal names the number, so that a design-tool export with a longer or larger one says which to trim. On output every number, in attributes, `d` and `points` alike, is printed from the parsed value rounded to 4 decimals with trailing zeros and a trailing point dropped, and never as the raw `f64`, so it is at most 12 characters (a sign, six digits, the point and four decimals). A number that rounds to zero prints `0`, never `-0` (`-0.00001` is `0`), so that the output parses back to the number it prints. The read side of section 4 runs this same parse on the stored text and serves the printer's output, never the stored text. The printer is idempotent: printing the parse of its own output gives the same bytes, which a property test holds (section 13); nothing on the read side depends on it.
- The `d` of a path and the `points` of a polygon are tokenised and re-emitted: command letters of `MmLlHhVvCcSsQqTtAaZz` and numbers (the two flags of an arc are read as flags), separated by one space, at most 8 KiB each. Anything else in them is refused. The input tokenizer accepts the compact grammar of SVG: spaces and commas separate, a sign or a second point starts a new number (`10-5` is 10 and -5, `.5.5` is .5 and .5), and each arc flag is a single `0` or `1` that may be packed with the next number (`a1 1 0 011 1`); the output is always space separated.
- Limits: input over 16 KiB or canonical text over 16 KiB is refused. Input must be UTF-8; a byte order mark or any other encoding is refused with a named reason. The canonical text is a single line made only of the bytes `A-Z a-z 0-9` and space `, . : / # ( ) = < > " -`; the generated-input test of section 13 asserts that set and that every `"` is the delimiter of an attribute value that the writer produced.
- Read-back and bounds. The reader holds what it accepts to what the read side will accept: it prints the parse once and reads that text again, and refuses the file with the code `svg_canonical`, naming what the second reading refused, when the printed text would not pass. That catches a `viewBox` width or height that rounds to zero at 4 decimals (`0.00004` prints as `0`), and a compact `d` or `points` that, written out in full with one space between two items (`.5.5` is four bytes, `0.5 0.5` seven), prints longer than 8 KiB. Before it applies any rule about the content it refuses elements nested more than 16 levels deep, the root included, and more than 32 attributes on one element. It reports at most 20 refusals of one file, none twice, so a hostile file cannot make a long answer.
- Refused with a named reason: `<script>`, `<style>`, `<foreignObject>`, `<image>`, `<use>`, `<text>`, `<a>`, `<defs>`, `<polyline>`, gradients, patterns, clip paths, filters, masks, animation, a DOCTYPE, an entity or `&`, CDATA, a comment, a processing instruction other than the first line, any attribute not in the list above (`style`, `class`, `id`, `opacity`, `stroke-dasharray`, `on*`, `href`, `xlink:*`, `xmlns:*`), any other `xmlns` than the SVG namespace, any URL or `url(`, a second root. A design tool's comment, `id`s and `<defs>` are the usual reasons a file is refused; the reason names the element, so the fix is to delete it.
- `currentColor` takes the text colour on the server-rendered pages and is black in an `<img>` (the back office); a logo for both light and dark uses explicit colours.

**Escaping.** `name`, titles and messages are written to HTML text with the existing `esc` (`page.rs:81-92`; the signer page imports it, `K/obop-http/src/sign_page.rs:17`), to attributes with `shell::attribute`, to JSON with `serde_json`, to the tab title with `esc`. The file name uses only the slug. The generated tile is not stored artwork: the server writes it from a template and puts one validated scalar value into its `<text>`, escaped (section 2). The reader re-checks every field (section 4), so a row written around the command cannot reach a page raw.

## 9. Caching

- Server-rendered pages already send `no-store` (`K/obop-http/src/oauth.rs:81`, `sign.rs:428`, `connectors.rs:128`); a change shows on the next render of that page. The brand they read comes from the reader's cache (section 4): a change shows at once in the process that made it and within two seconds in another process on the same database. The public site and shop add their own 5-second cache in front (section 4), so the server's answer for a shop page can lag by up to 7 seconds (a browser or a shared cache in front of the shop may keep a page 60 seconds more and a stale copy 300 more, as section 14 says), and a browser keeps the shop's own tile favicon for a day (`K/obop-site/src/seo.rs:80`); the logo route of this record, and the shop's favicon when it is the record's artwork, are `no-cache`.
- The shell is `no-cache`; the meta tag, title and icon link are made per request from the cached brand.
- The logo route is `no-cache` with an `ETag` that is `"<v>"`, and its URL carries `?v=<v>`, so a new logo, or a new accent under a generated tile, is a new URL and a revalidating client does not keep the old bytes. Browsers hold tab icons for a long time regardless; a changed logo may show in an old tab icon until the browser drops it (section 14). With no brand set the route answers the layout's 404, which carries no `Cache-Control` (section 4).
- Cost, while the store answers: at most one read of the row and one of the theme setting per process per two seconds for every caller of `current()`, however many page, shell, logo, discovery or OpenAPI requests arrive, because the refill is single-flight (section 4), apart from one more read when a configure or clear of this process lands while a refill runs. A refill that fails is not kept, so during an outage the cache is effectively off: each flight costs one failed read and the flights follow one another, while a caller that finds one running and has a last good brand serves it without waiting. `strict()` adds one read of the row per enrolment and `get` its own reads per call. A Slack message adds the two uncached reads of `fresh()`, the row and the theme setting, for each send (once for a manual send of several models, once for a test message, once for each try of an event, at most four), where the cell would have cost at most two in two seconds (S3; docs/ISSUES.md PS3-10); `initialize` over HTTP asks through `current()` and adds nothing to the cost of the cell. The logo route is a picture request that does ask the reader, through the cache; a request for a static script, style sheet or picture that the shell serves does not.
- The site keeps nothing that it built from a read that failed (S1f). When the settings row cannot be read, or the reader has no brand to give (a process that never had a good read, the store failing: `Shown::is_unread`, for the request that ran the read and for the ones that waited for it alike), `PublicApi::site_settings_read` says `degraded`, and the site serves that object to the request that asked and keeps none of it: the next request reads again, and when the store answers the shop is what the store holds, with the owner's explicit `site.*` keys, not after the 5 seconds. The request that met the fault is the one D74 answers 503 when the fault is one of its classes (`storage_busy`, `storage_unavailable`, `storage_timeout`); a read error of another kind (a table that is missing, say) is not one, and the request that met it is served the stand-in, as it was before this slice (the logo route answers it as an instance with no brand does), and the site keeps none of it, but the stand-in is sent with the shop's usual `Cache-Control` and so may be kept by a browser or a shared cache like any shop page. A brand that was read once and is served as the last good one while the store fails is a brand that was read, and the site keeps it for its cache time like any good answer. The menu is read in the same refresh and is held to the same rule: a published menu that could not be read is empty for the request that asked and is not kept, while a shop that publishes no menu has an empty menu that is kept like any answer. Nothing is kept to fail fast with, so while the store fails each shop request reads again, one after another (the site's cache is locked while it reads); not measured on PostgreSQL.

## 10. Anti-phishing: what a brand setting cannot change

The consent page tells the person that the application registered itself and that the instance cannot verify who runs or made it, and shows where the result goes (`{host}`, or the phone scheme). That is the protection against a hostile client. The record may change how the page names itself, not what it says about the client. Fixed, whatever the row says:

1. The "Who is asking" box, its heading and its sentences are never read from the record. The neutral twins keep the claim ("This instance cannot verify who runs {app}"); no field can shorten, hide or reword it. A test fails if a consent page with a brand lacks the box, the host line or the second mention of `{app}` in any language (section 13); that each language still says the right thing is the per-language review of section 7, not a test.
2. The client's name is shown as it registered, escaped, in `<strong>`, and the brand name is never put in the sentence that names the client or on a button. Under a brand the name carries more weight than `obop` did: it is the organisation an employee trusts, and dynamic registration of public clients is open to anyone who can reach the instance (`K/obop-oauth/src/client.rs:174-178` accepts any name of up to 80 characters), so a hostile client can register under the brand's name. The defence is two fixed things, neither read from the record. (a) A client whose name equals the brand `name`, the effective slug or any `title_<lang>` that is set, after both sides are folded the same way, gets one more sentence in the box, `consent.same_name` (section 7): "This name is the same as the name of this instance. That does not mean that the application belongs to this instance." The name and the titles are what a person reads at the top of the page, which shows `title_<lang>` or else `name` (section 6, the OAuth pages row), and the slug is the instance's other name; all the set titles are compared, so the answer does not depend on the language of the page. Registration is not refused, because that would change the protocol's behaviour and the genuine app may register under the brand's name; if the genuine MOHV app does, it carries the sentence too, so its client name, a value of the identity block (S4), should not equal the brand name, its slug or any of its titles. The fold, applied to the client name, the brand `name`, the slug and every title that is set alike: remove every character with the property Default_Ignorable_Code_Point or the general category `Cf` and all white space (`char::is_whitespace`, the `White_Space` property, no-break space included), then NFKC, then `str::to_lowercase`. A client name that is empty after the fold equals no name. The comparison does not rely on the cleaning that registration does: the name is stored as `clean_text` left it, cut to 80 characters (`K/obop-oauth/src/util.rs:81-104`, applied at `client.rs:174`), and `clean_text` drops only the list of `devices::is_invisible` (`K/obop-commands/src/devices.rs:56-86`), which stops at U+FE0E (U+FE0F, the variation selector 16, is kept, and so are U+FFF0 to U+FFF8 and the default-ignorable code points after U+E01EF) and turns each stretch of white space into one ordinary space. A client registered as the brand name with one invisible character or a no-break space inside it therefore still gets the sentence. The fold is a stated approximation of case folding (section 14). (b) The host or scheme line and the "cannot verify" sentence stay. A name that only contains the brand (`MOHV Support`) is not equal and gets no extra sentence; for it the defence is (b) alone, as for any other name an attacker picks. A branded consent page is therefore less protective than the stock one against a client that copies the brand; this is a stated limit (section 14).
3. The Microsoft and passkey buttons, the names of third parties (Microsoft, Google) and the connector provider names are never replaced by the brand.
4. Only a person who holds `platform.admin` can write the record (one wrapper, section 3); every changed field of the record is in the security log with its old and new value (the artwork as its version; a value that is not text, and a key that this binary does not know, without its value; a long or unsafe value cut or escaped; section 3), written in the same atomic batch as the change. The theme and the `site.*` keys that the record defers to are not covered by this log: `setting.update` records only changes under `security` (section 14).
5. The character policy of section 2 applies when `configure` writes: no control, format, bidirectional, zero-width or other default-ignorable character, no markup, none of the characters or forms that make a link or an address, no mixed-script name. On every read the read check of section 4 applies: the safety subset for text, the parse and the re-print for the artwork. Messages are plain text with no link markup. An uploaded logo cannot contain script, text or a reference to another resource; the generated tile is the server's own template with one escaped glyph.
6. What a message may say is the operator's business. The policy keeps out the plain forms of a link, an address and a phone number (section 2); that is a lint of obvious forms, not a guarantee that a message cannot send a visitor somewhere. The text is shown escaped on the instance's own pages, with no link markup; the form tells the administrator that the text is readable by anyone.
7. Enrolment fails closed (section 4): the authenticator label and the passkey name are never written from a default that a read error substituted.

## 14. Migration, rollout, hosting and honest limits

**Migration.** None for an instance that sets nothing. A new model adds rows only; records are JSON documents, so there is no DDL and no data step. Backups and restores carry the row as they carry any record; a restore of a backup made before the feature leaves the row absent. A binary without the feature has no code that reads the row. `CORE_VERSION` is not changed (S2 bumps the `sign` package, not the core). `docs/DEPLOY.md` gets a section when S1 lands, with the rollout order below (configure the brand before the first enrolment included), and the line for `OBOP_MFA_ISSUER` there (`docs/DEPLOY.md:73`) states the precedence. The section also says how an administrator who must still enrol repairs a branding row that cannot be read: `obop admin brand configure` or `obop admin brand clear` at the terminal (section 4).

**Rollout for MOHV.** Order matters. (1) Before the first enrolment of anyone, run `obop admin brand configure --name MOHV` (from S1; with `--hide-product-docs on` once S2 has landed), because the two-factor enrolment that D53 requires of administrators by default writes the authenticator label then, and a passkey writes its relying-party name when it is made, and neither can be corrected afterwards. On a provisioned instance the first administrator is created at provisioning with a password (`K/obop-platform/src/provision.rs:6-17`; `InstanceSpec` is the slug, the owner's address, the password hash, the update token and the control URL, with no brand field and no environment), and D53 makes that person enrol at the first sign-in in a limited session that runs no command. So the operator runs the CLI, which needs no sign-in, before that administrator first signs in, or sets `OBOP_MFA_ISSUER` by hand in the service environment before that sign-in (the provisioner passes a fixed list of variables, `K/obop-platform/src/sliplane.rs:444-473`). `OBOP_MFA_ISSUER` covers the authenticator label only: a passkey registered at the first sign-in takes its relying-party name from the row through `strict()`, and with no row it writes `obop` to the device, so the terminal step is the only way to get both right. An entry made earlier keeps its old label or name; `obop admin reset-mfa` removes the authenticator and the passkeys (`K/obop-auth/src/mfa.rs:739`), and a new enrolment replaces them. (2) Choose the accent in Customize, Theme (the existing instance theme, not the record), and choose one other than the default violet. Until then the generated "M" tile is already the neutral slate, while the sign-in screens and the back office keep the stock violet; pressing "Set as the default for everyone" without picking a colour stores the default violet, so the screens stay violet and the tile stays slate (section 2). (3) Swapping to the real logo later is `obop admin brand configure --logo-file logo.svg`, which changes nothing else; `obop admin brand check logo.svg` says first what to remove.

The record is not the only thing MOHV sets. Still outside it: the host name, which is derived from the slug for a provisioned instance (the service name `obop-<slug>`, `K/obop-platform/src/sliplane.rs:251`) or set by `OBOP_PUBLIC_URL`, and the passkey `rp.id` that follows it; the phone app's build identity (S4); the release feed, whose default is `https://obop.dev/api/releases` and which the operator can switch off with `OBOP_UPDATE_FEED=off` (`K/obop-update/src/updater.rs:58-62`, `K/obop-update/src/release.rs:16`, `docs/DEPLOY.md:43`) at the price of a standing warning on the Updates tab (`updater.rs:315-318`), which is the operator's own choice and no question here; the names chosen outside obop (the Entra app registration, the Google OAuth client, the connector name typed in Claude); and MOHV's own manifests, Storybook sentence and image metadata. Until S1 lands, `OBOP_MFA_ISSUER=MOHV` and the `site.*` keys (`obop site set`) already do part of this with no code (the audit's S0).

**Hosting and the sandbox.** The record is per instance: one database and one service per instance (`K/obop-platform/src/sliplane.rs:1-2`), and the provisioning request `InstanceSpec` has no brand field (`K/obop-platform/src/provision.rs:6-17`), so one instance cannot read or change another's brand and nothing here reaches across instances. On the hosted offerings of D83 the owner of an instance is an operator of pages on someone else's domain. The free sandbox gives each visitor a short-lived business with an administrator on a SpaceCorps address. **The risk, plainly:** a visitor who is administrator of their own sandbox instance can put a name, a message and a logo on that instance's sign-in and signer pages on a SpaceCorps address. D83's nine duties (`docs/DECISIONS.md:1178`: terms, privacy notice, abuse and rate controls with a kill switch, egress and mail blocking, auto-reset, sanctions screening, cost, on-call burden, counsel review) are about the sandbox as a service, none about the content an administrator of a sandbox puts on a page, and D83 allows no SpaceCorps-only code path (`DECISIONS.md:1180`). The same administrator already controls the text of a signer page through the request and document titles, which are escaped but arbitrary (`K/obop-http/src/sign_page.rs:140-190`). The owner was asked whether to build a general operator lock, an environment switch that makes `configure` and `clear` refuse; he declined it on 2026-11-05 (Q71 item 11, answered NO LOCK, relayed from his card). No lock is built, in any slice, and D83 is not amended. He accepted the risk without one; it is to be revisited if the sandbox opens. The item asked about the free sandbox. The same risk exists on a hosted plan (OR-22) whose administrator is the customer, and this spec extends the statement to it as its own: that extension is not part of the answer, the owner is told when the answers go into the register (15.3 (g)), and its revisit trigger is the first such instance that is provisioned. A white-label operator also cannot declare a brand required: a `clear`, or a restore from a backup made before the feature, shows the default name again, and enrolments then carry the label `obop` until the brand is set again.

### 15.4 What reading the code for S1 added

A read of the code to plan slice S1 found twelve facts that the text did not state. Five changed the design and are written into the sections named; the others are build facts, two lines each. None changes the answer to an item of Q71.

1. **The tile could not call the shop's colour code** (sections 2 and 4). `obop-site` depends on `obop-commands`, so a leaf crate that `obop-commands` uses cannot depend on it; the pure colour maths moves into `obop-brand` and `obop-site` re-exports it, and the shop pins show the bytes did not move.
2. **Where the Reader lives** (section 4). The `Dispatcher` holds it as an `OnceLock`, created by `register_admin`; the many constructors of `HttpState`, `PublicApi` and `Auth` keep their signatures, and `Auth` takes only the uncached `brand::strict`.
3. **The shop's favicon needs a channel for the artwork** (sections 4 and 5). The shop's `Settings` is fed by `site_settings_read` only (`site_settings` wraps it), so the artwork goes, with its `v`, through one reserved key of its map, stripped from the stored `site` object first because `setting.update` writes any key.
4. **A failed brand read meets D74** (sections 4 and 14). The request that runs the failed read is answered 503 by `ops::storage_faults`; the Reader serves the last good brand, without waiting, to a caller that finds a refill running and has one (a caller with none waits for the flight), and never keeps a failure.
5. **`AuthConfig.issuer` was a `String`** (section 5). It becomes an `Option<String>` with one shared `brand::env_issuer()`, so `get` can name `env` and `OBOP_MFA_ISSUER=obop` can beat a record.
6. **CLI flags collide** (section 3). `--title`, `--message` and `--dry-run` are global in `main.rs`; `admin brand` takes its repeatable flags first, and a test keeps `status incident create --title T` working.
7. **The platform module needs labels** (sections 7 and 12). The D52 tests (`crates/obop/src/main.rs:4111` and `:4141`) require every built-in module in six languages: about 336 admin-facing, machine-drafted cells (348 with S2) in `packages/labels/<lang>/platform.toml`.
8. **Two committed generated files move** (section 13). The new kinds change the description of `security_event.kind`, so `openapi.json` and `schema.d.ts` are regenerated and two gates enforce it; the words for the kinds in the back office list are S2.
9. **The generated `get` description is wrong for this model** (section 3). The registry writes "Read one record by id" (`K/obop-registry/src/lib.rs:453`); S1 adds a describe hook for `instance_brand` like the one of `import_map` (`K/obop-commands/src/import_map.rs:99`, applied at `K/obop-commands/src/lib.rs:748`).
10. **No new crate for tests or case folding** (sections 2 and 10). `icu_normalizer` and `icu_properties` become direct dependencies; `icu_casemap` and a property-test crate are not in `Cargo.lock` and are not added: the fold of section 10 (NFKC and `str::to_lowercase`, after the removal of ignorable characters and white space), and a fixed-seed generator.
11. **The path of the logo route was not an unknown path before S1b** (section 4). The path matched `/api/{model}/{command}`, which takes `POST` only, so in every layout a `GET` answered 405 with an empty body and a `POST` reached `call` (401 or 404); S1b changes both on purpose, and with them the answer to a `HEAD` (a 405 before) and the `Allow` of a cross-origin preflight (`POST` before). With no brand the `GET` gets the answer the layout gives an unknown one-segment path of the API (the JSON with a code and a hint beside the API alone and the back office, the short `{"error":"not found"}` in the public site's router), and with one the artwork; a `POST` gets 405 with `Allow: GET,HEAD`, which the first test run of S1b measured and pinned.
12. **Inline SVG against the style-hash policy is untried** (section 14). If the logo sits badly on the baseline, the fix is a second constant style sheet with its own hash, added to `style-src` only when a brand is set; that would amend section 8.
