## 1. Purpose and scope

### 1.1 What this area is

obop is a web product: the back office and the PWA are clients of the REST and MCP surface. A PWA cannot keep receiving location updates while the phone is locked (general knowledge), so one use case needs a native client: recording a trip for a mileage report. OR-34 allows React Native (Expo) apps as obop clients. D87 proposes to limit them to first-party apps and to cases a PWA cannot serve (Proposed), and names Mileage as the first. This document specifies that app and the `mileage` package behind it, plus the general rules a later native app would follow.

An app is a client and nothing more. It has no private backdoor: it calls REST and the OpenAPI document, signs in through OAuth, and meets the checks of the one Dispatcher like every other client (D80). Business logic (distance, validation, state changes, permissions) lives on the server; the app captures, buffers and uploads.

## 2. Personas and roles

## 4. Background location

### 4.1 The options

Sources read by the lead session (verified): the Expo SDK 58 Location documentation, the Android documentation on foreground service types, the Google Play background-location policy and the Transistorsoft README. The Expo pages were those of SDK 58, not SDK 57; SDK 57 is assumed to behave the same (unverified). Apple's documentation page did not render, so Apple's own rules for iOS (what a locked iPhone does with an app that has "When In Use" access, the 7 day profile, the local network prompt) are general knowledge; what 4.2 says about `expo-location` on iOS was read from the installed package and run in the iOS Simulator (8.8), and no part of it was run on an iPhone.

| Option | What it is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `expo-location` with `expo-task-manager` | `startLocationUpdatesAsync(task, options)` with a top-level `TaskManager.defineTask`; on Android the `foregroundService` option (notification title and body required); config plugin flags `isIosBackgroundLocationEnabled`, `isAndroidBackgroundLocationEnabled` and `isAndroidForegroundServiceEnabled`. It needs a development build, not Expo Go. Limits from the documentation (verified): updates stop if the user terminates the app; Android does not restart a terminated app on a location event; vendor behaviour varies (the documentation links dontkillmyapp.com); `requestBackgroundPermissionsAsync` is "Always" on iOS and "Allow Once" makes it fail silently; on Android 11 and later it opens the system settings page; foreground permission must be granted first. Free, no licence key. | **The proposed default path of the first slice (D87).** Gap: no motion or stationary detection, so continuous GPS drains the battery; mitigate with accuracy, a distance filter and deferred updates (the documentation says deferred updates save battery) and measure on real phones. |
| `react-native-background-geolocation` (Transistorsoft) | Mature, battery-aware, with a built-in SQLite buffer and HTTP uploader. The repository is MIT, but release builds on iOS and Android need a paid licence key (a 30-day trial for release builds; the price is not on the page). | Excluded as the default (OR-34 d). The reason (obop is completely free to build and self-host, OR-1 and OR-2, and a paid key in the build path of the flagship app breaks that) and the remark that an optional adapter later is not forbidden are the proposal's (Proposed). |
| A Capacitor plugin | Not React Native. A prior mileage app used one: it worked on a simulator, then its first real drive exposed seven bugs. | Evidence only, not an option |
| An own native module (Kotlin FusedLocationProvider foreground service, Swift CLLocationManager) as an Expo module | Maximum control, highest cost, two languages to maintain. | The proposed fallback if `expo-location` proves unreliable in the field spike. FusedLocationProvider is part of Google Play services, the same dependency that `expo-location` has on Android (8.3, Q43); a module that avoids it uses the platform `LocationManager`. ### 4.2 Platform rules that shape the design

- **Android 14 and later (verified).** A foreground service of type `location` needs `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION` and `android:foregroundServiceType="location"`; `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` or `ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` must be granted; and a location foreground service cannot be started from the background unless the app holds `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION`. Consequence: a trip started by a tap while the app is visible works without any background permission; a trip that starts by itself (geofence, car Bluetooth) needs the background permission and the Play declaration. That is a further reason, by scope, to leave automatic start out (5.9); automatic start is not ruled by OR-34, which covers only auto-stop.
- **Google Play (verified).** Background location is allowed only for core functionality; the Permissions Declaration Form in Play Console; one background-location feature; a short demonstration video of the Android app (30 seconds or less recommended); an in-app disclosure before the runtime prompt that contains the word "location" and a background phrase such as "when the app is closed or not in use", also in the store listing and on the website. Deadlines were not found.
- **Google Play and the foreground service (read again by the spike session on 2026-10-27, from the raw page text of the policy, section "Foreground Service (FGS) api"; `support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150`).** The page makes location through a foreground service subject to review. It says the foreground service must be started as a continuation of an in-app, user-initiated action, and must be "terminated immediately after the application completes the intended use case" of that action (one quote, kept short on purpose; look up the full sentence at the page). It also says that a use of location through a foreground service that is equivalent to `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION`, or otherwise location in the background, falls under the requirements for location in the background, and those include the permission declaration process (paraphrased). **Still unverified, and important:** the page does not say whether a trip that a driver starts by a tap and that records for hours counts as a use "equivalent" to the background permission, and it gives no example of a trip-recording app. **Plan (an assumption, not a finding):** assume that a store release needs the declaration form and the demonstration video; assume that an internal test build or a sideloaded APK does not (this was not read from the policy either). Q37 decides what is done before any listing is made.
- **iOS (read in the installed `expo-location` 58.0.18 on 2026-10-29 and run in the iOS Simulator the same day; not run on an iPhone; 8.8).** `UIBackgroundModes: location` is added only by the config option `isIosBackgroundLocationEnabled`, which the app sets; the `Info.plist` of the built app holds `["location"]` and no other background mode (read with `plutil`, 8.8). Starting background updates checks the "When In Use" permission only (the code says that the two start modes cannot be told apart) and `EXLocationTaskConsumer.m` sets `allowsBackgroundLocationUpdates = YES` itself, so a tap-to-start trip asks for no "Always" permission, and the app ships no "Always", motion or temporary-full-accuracy text (lead decision of the port; the owner can override it). The stable API of the module has no `CLBackgroundActivitySession`; `expo-location/next` has one. **Whether a "When In Use" app keeps receiving positions with the screen locked for 30 minutes is decided by the iPhone test of 9.2 and by nothing else (NEEDS-DEVICE): the Expo documentation says "Always" and the installed source does not.** What the Simulator showed: with the Home button pressed the app was in the state "running in the background" and kept storing points (9 to 31 points in 46 seconds); the Simulator does not model a locked screen. A terminated app is not relaunched under "When In Use" (general knowledge); force-quit stops updates on both platforms (Expo documentation, verified). If the iPhone test fails, the fallback, in order, is `expo-location/next` with `CLBackgroundActivitySession` (iOS 17 and later), then "Always" with the two-step prompt; each changes this section and 5.6, and neither is built: it needs an owner ruling first.
- **Battery.** Continuous high-accuracy GPS for a drive is expensive. Vendor battery killers on Android are the main field risk; the app needs a "keep this app running" guide, and the first real-phone test measures drain over a 30-minute drive (9, acceptance item 5).
- **Versions (unverified: seen in a search result, the page was not read).** An upgrade guide dated 2026-10-21 names SDK 58 as current with React Native 0.88, six days before 2026-10-27; SDK 57 is React Native 0.86. The owner chose SDK 57 for now (OR-34 e) and on 2026-10-28 amended it: SDK 58 if its packages pass the cooldown when the app's build starts, otherwise SDK 57 and a move at the first clean point (1.2). The app pins SDK 58 since 2026-10-28 (8.7).

## 5. The mileage package

Built on 2026-10-28 (8.6) as a **built-in Rust module**, `crates/modules/mileage`, like `crm` and `sales`, and not as the package `packages/mileage.toml` that this section first Proposed. The lead session decided that the eight capabilities of 5.8 are not built as generic kernel features for the first slice. The models, the governed state, the seven actions, the three permission groups and every rule that the generated commands cannot state are in that crate, and they hold on REST, MCP and the command line alike because the dispatcher is the one path of all three. Where this section says Proposed, it is the design; where it says built, the crate does it and 8.6 names the test.

### 5.1 Models (names Proposed)

| Model | Fields and notes |
|---|---|
| `mileage_trip` | `id`: assigned by the server, like every record: `create` ignores a supplied `id` (it mints a UUIDv7) and `bulk_create` refuses one (D70). `client_trip_id`: text, a random UUID (version 4) made on the device at Start, **unique per driver** (`unique_with` `driver`, D57); the device's handle on the trip before it knows the server `id`, and the idempotency handle of the trip's creation (5.3). `driver`: the user (the `own` field, D58; it defaults to the creator). `vehicle`: optional, a `fleet_vehicle` of the bundled `fleet` package (which has `odometer_km`). `purpose`: business, private or commute; a private trip keeps only its distance, or is dropped (open, 15). `started_at` and `ended_at`: D72 `Datetime`. `distance_m`: a D72 decimal **computed on the server from the points, never trusted from the client**. `expected_points` and `last_seq`: numbers the device sets before `end` (5.3). `point_count` and `max_seq`: computed from the child points (D57). `discard_reason` and `reject_reason`: text set by `update` before `discard` or `reject`; who may write each, and when, is in 5.7. `confirmed_at`: a D72 `Datetime` set when the trip is confirmed. `integrity`: flags set by the validator (5.4). `state`: a governed select (D48) with the options in 5.2. |
| `mileage_point` | `trip` (a reference to the server `id` of the trip), `seq`, `recorded_at`, `lat`, `lon`, `accuracy_m`, `speed_mps`; unique on (`trip`, `seq`) (`unique_with`, D57). Written through `bulk_create` (5.3). It must be append-only: no update or delete after creation except by the retention job (5.6), and no new point once its trip has left `recording`; that is a requirement on the Proposed immutability of D77 and on a state-conditioned write rule (5.8), not something the generated `update` and `delete` already guarantee. |

### 5.2 The state machine

`state` is a governed field (D48): `create` starts at the first option, `recording`, and only the actions below move it, each with its own group and approval. The proposal names the states and some actions; the arrows, the groups, `discard` and `discard_stuck` are this document's reading (Proposed). An action takes no inputs (MODULES.md 3.4), so a value it needs (a count, a reason) is a field set by `update` first, and the action asserts on it (D56).

| Action | From | To | Who | Assertion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `end` | `recording` | `ended` | the driver | the points are complete (`point_count` equals `expected_points` and `max_seq` equals `last_seq`) and `distance_m` is above 0 |
| `submit` | `ended` | `in_review` | the driver | none beyond the state |
| `confirm` | `in_review` | `confirmed` | `mileage.reviewer`, not the driver of the trip (5.7) | none; sets `confirmed_at` |
| `reject` | `in_review` | `rejected` | `mileage.reviewer` | `reject_reason` is not empty |
| `reopen` | `rejected` | `ended` | `mileage.reviewer`, so the driver can correct and submit again | none |
| `discard` | `recording`, `ended` or `rejected` | `discarded` | the driver | `discard_reason` is not empty |
| `discard_stuck` | `recording` or `ended` | `discarded` | `mileage.admin` | none; the audit log records who discarded the trip |

`discarded` is terminal: the record and its audit trail stay, and it never counts. An action has one group, and groups imply nothing, so the administrator's discard is its own action and not `discard` with a second group; the administrator needs no right to write `discard_reason`. A package action takes one `from` value (MODULES.md 3.4), so an action with several source states is declared once per source state, or without `from` and with an assertion on `state`; that is a package detail. Agents can only propose `confirm`, `reject`, `discard` and `discard_stuck` (approval for agents, D18, D48). Whether a mistakenly confirmed trip can go back to `in_review` is open (15). A month lock is not an action of a trip and is out of scope for the first slice (5.9).

**Built (8.6).** The seven actions exist as declared. Their handlers are the module's own (`handlers.rs`), and each one is a single compare-and-set write of the trip: the stored state is checked again inside the write, so two callers cannot both move the same trip, and a state that changed meanwhile is refused with `state_conflict`. Where the built module differs from the table: `end` is refused by the handler and not by a generated assertion, and the same write stores `distance_m` (5.4); `end` takes `expected_points` and `last_seq` in the call, or reads them from the trip when the device wrote them with `update` first; `confirm` sets `confirmed_at` (the clock of the server) and `reviewed_by` in the write that sets the state, and `reject` sets `reviewed_by`; `submit` clears an old `reject_reason`; `discard_stuck` writes the log entry `mileage_discard_stuck` in the same atomic batch as the state change (the entry exists exactly when the discard does, and a refused batch leaves neither). `mileage.reviewer` and `mileage.admin` carry no right to use the models by themselves, and the module enforces it: a role counts only for a principal that also holds `mileage.driver`, the base group of both models. The guard of an action decides the caller's roles once (`Who::of`) and stamps the result into the input under the `_actor_mileage_*` keys, which the dispatcher strips from client input, and the handler reads those keys, so the guard and the handler cannot disagree. The same rule holds for the approver of an agent's proposal: `Dispatcher::approve` stamps the approver's id, admin flag and usable groups under `_actor_approver*`, and the guard refuses an approver without `mileage.driver` (a trip it names answers as a missing record) and an approver without the role the action needs, before the plan is spent, so the plan stays pending for someone who may decide it. The discard an agent proposes for a driver is the driver's own act: only the driver of the trip may approve it (a driver who is not the owner is told there is no such trip, a reviewer or an administrator who reads it is told whose action it is), because a discard by a stranger would leave no record that names them. An account with only `mileage.reviewer` or only `mileage.admin` therefore sees no trip and cannot act, directly or by approving.

### 5.3 Writing a trip from the device

**What `bulk_create` idempotency delivers today (D70, checked in `crates/kernel/obop-commands/src/engine/bulk.rs`).** The key is scoped to the person, the model and the key text (1 to 128 visible ASCII characters). A repeat of the identical request (same rows, same mode) within 24 hours returns the first answer, which lists the created ids, marked `replayed: true`, and creates nothing; the answer is stored in the same transaction as the rows. The same key with different rows or another mode is refused. After 24 hours the key is forgotten, and the same call creates its rows again unless something else refuses them. A dry run neither reads nor stores a key. No other command of the dispatcher has an idempotency key (single `create`, `update` and actions have none); the public-shop checkout and the payment calls have their own, outside the command surface. Limits: at most 1,000 rows a call, and by default 12 calls a minute per person (`OBOP_BULK_PER_MINUTE`). The answer to a repeat is looked up before the minute's counter, so a retry of an answered call never uses up the budget and is never refused for it; a new call over the limit is refused with the code `rate_limited` (HTTP 429, `Retry-After: 60`, nothing changed; MODULES.md 6.2).

**The design (Proposed).**

1. **Start.** The device makes a random UUID (version 4) as `client_trip_id`, not a version 7: the first 48 bits of a UUIDv7 are the time it was made, so it would carry the start instant of the trip, and an agent's token reads `client_trip_id` in the clear while `started_at` is withheld from it (ISSUES M11). It stores the trip locally, and queues `mileage_trip.bulk_create` with one row and `idempotency_key = "trip:" + client_trip_id`. Creating the trip through `bulk_create` and not `create` is what gives it an idempotency key. A retry inside 24 hours returns the first answer with the server `id`. A retry after that is refused by the unique constraint on (`driver`, `client_trip_id`), and the app then finds the trip with `list` filtered by `client_trip_id` and takes its `id`.
2. **Points.** Point batches wait until the `id` of the trip is known, because `mileage_point.trip` is a reference to it. Each batch is a `mileage_point.bulk_create` with `idempotency_key = "points:" + client_trip_id + ":" + first_seq + "-" + last_seq`. A batch's rows and range never change once it is queued, so a replay is identical. A replay after 24 hours is refused by the unique constraint on (`trip`, `seq`) and, because the mode is `all_or_nothing`, writes nothing; the outbox treats that refusal as "already stored" after checking the trip's `point_count` and `max_seq`. The app sends batches of many points about once a minute, inside the rate limit, and not point by point. A batch that was stored is answered again with the first answer (`replayed: true`) whatever has happened to the trip since: the module lets a repeat with an unexpired stored answer through even when the trip has ended or is near the cap (built, 8.6), so a lost answer to the last batch does not strand the outbox. A batch with a new key keeps every check.
3. **Stop.** The device queues, behind the outstanding batches, an `update` of the trip (`ended_at`, `expected_points`, `last_seq`) and then `end`. A repeat of `update` is harmless, and a repeat of `end` after success is refused by the state check; the outbox reads the trip and treats `ended` or a later state as done.
4. **Incomplete.** While points are still missing, `end` is refused with the code `incomplete` (built, 8.6: HTTP 409, no `Retry-After`, body `{"error", "code": "incomplete", "hint"}`). It is returned only when fewer points are stored than the device said and none beyond `last_seq` is stored (a gap in the middle counts: points 1, 2, 3 and 5 stored for `expected_points` 5 and `last_seq` 5 is incomplete until point 4 arrives), so that more points could still arrive. Nothing was changed, and the same call can be repeated; if the missing points were lost on the device and will never arrive, the driver repeats `end` with `expected_points` and `last_seq` corrected to what exists (the hint says so). When the trip already holds more points than `expected_points`, a higher `seq` than `last_seq`, or the right count with another `last_seq` (also a `last_seq` above the highest stored `seq`), or when `expected_points` is above `last_seq` or either number is above 20,000 (a trip holds at most 20,000 points), no point that arrives can make the numbers fit: that is a plain 400 for good whose text names the stored values (`expected_points {n} and last_seq {m}`), and the driver updates the numbers and ends again. A refusal for good (a wrong state, a trip with no distance, no counts) is a plain 400 without that code, so the outbox repeats a 409 `incomplete` with backoff and treats any other 4xx as final until the driver discards the trip or corrects the numbers. A repeat of `end` after it succeeded is a plain 400 too (the state is no longer `recording`); the outbox reads the trip first, as step 3 says.
5. **Submit.** `submit` is a command like the others and queues behind `end`.

If the phone has no connection at Stop, nothing is lost: everything waits in the outbox, and the trip stays `recording` on the server until `end` succeeds. A trip that stays `recording` for hours is flagged (5.4) and can be discarded.

**Batch rate and a long trip (built, 8.6).** `bulk_create` keeps its limit of 12 calls a minute per person. A three-hour trip with a fix every 5 seconds is 2,160 points, which is three calls of up to 1,000 rows, far inside the limit; the size of a batch, not the rate, is what the app has to watch. A trip holds at most 20,000 points, kept by the range of `seq` (1 to 20,000; a number outside it is refused, and (`trip`, `seq`) is unique), so the guard of a batch reads nothing from the instance. A batch of 1,000 points took about 0.2 to 1.8 seconds in a debug build on an empty instance (memory and SQLite, 8.6). Until the keyed lookup of D57 (ISSUES M8) the unique check of the kernel looked each row up by a scan of every stored point of the instance, so the time of a batch grew with the number of points already stored, by every driver: with 5,000 points stored a batch of 1,000 took about 12.8 s on the memory backend and 4.8 s on SQLite, and with 20,000 stored about 68 s and 14 s, while the call holds the write lock. Since that change the check reads through the unique index of (`trip`, `seq`): the same test in a debug build took 0.22 s, 0.25 s and 0.30 s on SQLite with 0, 5,000 and 20,000 points stored. The memory backend still scans (12.8 s and 64.0 s at 5,000 and 20,000; it is for tests and `--dev`, ISSUES K37). The module itself does not scan (the cap is kept by the range of `seq`). No release-build figure was taken for the batch of this module, and on PostgreSQL the time of a batch is bounded by the latency of the statements (one lookup per row, ISSUES K36), which was measured with a test model of the kernel and not with this module.

### 5.4 Server-side validation

A prior mileage app fixed its logic in the client and enforced nothing on the server; one implausible segment there produced a trip of 8,666 km. The server therefore decides:

| Rule | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Speed cap | Reject a step above a cap; the prior app used 70 m/s (about 252 km/h). The value is a package constant, Proposed. The cap compares the times the phone wrote, so false client timestamps can defeat it (ISSUES M10). | An implausible jump must not count |
| Accuracy filter | Drop fixes whose `accuracy_m` is above a threshold. The threshold is open and set after the field test. | Poor fixes add distance |
| No zero-kilometre trips | `end` on a trip whose computed distance is 0 is refused for good with a reason; the driver `discard`s it. | A recorded trip is never saved as 0 km |
| End position | Taken from the last accepted fix. | The app may stop before the final upload |
| Duplicate batch | Within 24 hours the same request returns the first answer; later, the unique constraint refuses it (5.3). | Replays after a lost response |
| Sequence continuity | A gap in `seq` is flagged in `integrity`, not hidden; `end` also checks the counts. | Lost points are visible to the reviewer |
| Open too long | A trip that stays `recording` for hours is flagged. The number of hours is open. | A forgotten trip is the main risk of the manual baseline |
| Distance | Computed on the server from the accepted points with a geodesic helper. | The client's number is never trusted |

The validator and `trip-core` (8.1) share test vectors, so the device and the server agree on filtering and distance.

**Built (8.6).** The rules are two pure functions that agree, `crates/modules/mileage/src/track.rs` and `web/packages/trip-core/src/track.ts`, and both tests read the one file `web/packages/trip-core/src/track.fixtures.json` (13 cases: a walk, an antimeridian crossing, a jump over the cap, a gap in the signal, poor fixes, an unsorted track, a step back in time, standing still). Fixes are taken in the order of `seq`; a fix with `accuracy_m` above 100 m is dropped; a step above 70 m/s is skipped and the next fix is compared with the last accepted one (a step at exactly 70 m/s counts); the distance is the haversine distance on the mean Earth radius of 6,371,008.8 m, summed over the steps between accepted fixes and written with two decimals; the start and end position come from the first and the last accepted fix. The flags are `seq_gap:N`, `speed_jump:N` and `low_accuracy:N`, separated by spaces, empty for a clean track. `end` recomputes all of it from the stored points in the same write that sets the state, so a number from the device is never read; the speed cap and the accuracy limit are constants of the build, with the Proposed values, and are open item 5 of section 15. A trip whose accepted track has no distance, or that has no point at all, is refused for good.

### 5.5 What counts: the review gate

Totals, the export and the PDF count trips in state `confirmed`, grouped by the month of `confirmed_at` (in the platform zone `OBOP_ZONE`, D72). That is one predicate that every total, list total and export uses; a prior mileage app called it the `reviewed_at` gate. `in_review`, `rejected` and `discarded` trips never count. If a month lock is added later, it must not change which trips a total counts: a lock never removes a confirmed trip from the totals. The reviewer sees the integrity flags before confirming.

**Built (8.6).** The totals are the command `mileage_trip.search` with an `aggregate` over the trips in state `confirmed`, grouped by `confirmed_at` with `bucket` `month` (and `zone`, the platform zone `OBOP_ZONE` when none is named) and by `driver`, with the measure `sum:distance_m`, which the aggregate adds up exactly in the decimal scale of the field. That is one predicate for every total; the test places confirmations on either side of midnight at the end of September, in UTC and in Europe/Helsinki, and shows trips in every other state not counting. A person gets the totals of the trips they may read: a driver their own, a reviewer every driver's trips that are in review or decided, an administrator all. The back office cannot show these totals in its pivot or graph yet, because they group a datetime and add a decimal, which those views do not do (D72 "Not delivered"); the numbers are available to the API, MCP and the command line, and docs/ISSUES.md M3 lists it.

### 5.6 Privacy and retention

Not legal advice; obop supports compliance and is not a certification. The points that the research raised:

- Location on the device and in transit is a new processing location for the data-protection impact assessment.
- Raw points are minimised: the retention job (the one exception to "no update or delete of a point") thins or deletes them a number of days after a trip is confirmed, and the distance and, if needed, a simplified route stay. The number of days is open (15). Two other copies must follow the same rule: the encrypted outbox on the device (3) and the change history of the platform (D75), which can keep earlier versions of a record. For `mileage_point` the history entries are erased or thinned together with the point (`history_retention_days` and the erase operation of D75), and a test shows that no raw point survives in history after the retention date (Proposed). D75 history is not active yet, and while the interim class of 5.7 is `confidential_hr` the install gate refuses `history = "none"` for the model (MODULES.md 3.2), so history cannot simply be switched off.
- No third-party geocoder or map tiles by default (Q39); a prior mileage app sent exact coordinates to two public geocoding services. Start and end addresses are typed, or they come through a connector that passes the egress gate (D54).
- Every read of a route is audited (the audit log, [security-privacy.md](security-privacy.md) 4.4).
- Recording is active only while the driver started it and is visible: an Android notification, the iOS indicator.
- **An automatic trip end (auto-stop) ends a trip the driver did not end. The owner's condition is legal sign-off (OR-34 c); the proposal adds product and works-council sign-off. All three come first, and it stays out until then (Q41).** The prior research named guidance of the Swedish data-protection authority (IMY); it was not read here. Manual start and stop is the baseline, with the review gate and the open-trip warning as safeguards. Automatic start is not ruled and is out of scope (5.9).
- Tests use synthetic data only.

### 5.7 Permissions and data class

The proposal names no permission groups. The groups of section 2 and this matrix are this document's reading of it (Proposed).

| Model | Driver (`mileage.driver`) | Reviewer (`mileage.reviewer`) | Administrator (`mileage.admin`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mileage_trip` | create (through `bulk_create`) and read own; `update` own **only while the trip is `recording`, `ended` or `rejected`** and only the fields of the table below; no `delete` (the record is kept for audit, and `delete` is refused while points refer to the trip, D49); `in_review`, `confirmed` and `discarded` trips are read-only for the driver; actions `end`, `submit`, `discard` | read trips from `in_review` on (a state-conditioned read restriction, 5.8 item 2); `confirm`, `reject`, `reopen`; writes `reject_reason` while `in_review`. **Not the trips of which the reviewer is the driver** (separation of duties), unless an explicit policy of the business says otherwise | read all; `discard_stuck`; no other override of a `confirmed` trip |
| `mileage_point` | create through `bulk_create` and read own; no `update` or `delete` except by the retention job (5.6) | read, audited | read, audited |
| `device` | read, rename and revoke own | none | read and revoke any |

Built on 2026-10-28 (8.5): a person reads, renames and revokes their own devices with no group at all; the group that reads and revokes any is `api.device_admin` (Proposed in CONVENTIONS 4) together with the instance administrator, who rename only their own (a name is the owner's). The package of step 4 cannot change the row access of a built-in model, so `mileage.admin` gets this by being granted `api.device_admin`. The reviewer reads none. The rule for who may see a device is one function (`obop_commands::devices::may_see`) that the generated reads' row access (`own`, `see_all`) and the commands `get`, `list`, `search`, `update` and `revoke` all follow, by what kind of caller it is: the access token of the mobile app reads, renames and ends **its own device only** (it is given the id with its tokens, cannot list or search, and does not see its siblings); an AI agent's token only proposes the revoking of its owner's device. Tests over HTTP walk a matrix of the owner at the keyboard, the owner's app against its own and its sibling device, another person and their app, an agent of each, the device administrator and the instance administrator, against every command.

Who writes which field of a trip, and when (built, 8.6; each row is a test of the module):

| Field | Written by | Only while the trip is |
|---|---|---|
| `purpose`, `vehicle`, `started_at`, `ended_at`, `expected_points`, `last_seq` | the driver, by `update` | `recording`, `ended` or `rejected` |
| `discard_reason` | the driver, by `update`, before `discard` | `recording`, `ended` or `rejected` |
| `reject_reason` | the reviewer, by `update`, before `reject` | `in_review` |
| `integrity`, `distance_m`, `point_count`, `max_seq`, `confirmed_at`, `state` | nobody by `update`: the validator, a computation or an action sets them | never by `update` |

The reviewer group is distinct from the driver group. A person may hold both. The rule that they cannot confirm or reject their own trip is built (8.6): it is checked at the guard of the call and again in the handler, where the person who decides is the approver when an agent's proposal is being run, so neither a reviewer's own agent nor the reviewer approving a proposal on their own trip can decide it. `reopen` follows the same rule.

Data class: none of the existing classes fits location cleanly (`internal` is too weak, `special_category` too strong), so a new class `location` is Proposed, with masking for readers who need only the distance (Q40). CONVENTIONS section 5 already puts attendance geo in `confidential_hr`. Until Q40 is answered, the package declares `confidential_hr` for point fields (an interim reading of this document, not in the proposal); that class is not agent-readable by default and is excluded from the browser cache. Built (8.6): the fields of a trip that say who drove where and when follow the strictest class of what they are computed from or reveal (CONVENTIONS section 5; the precedent is `party.notes`, `internal` with personal data): `started_at` and `ended_at` are `confidential_hr` with personal data (they time the person's day, like an attendance), and `vehicle`, `purpose`, `reject_reason` and `discard_reason` are `internal` with personal data. An agent's token does not receive the fields of a sensitive class at all (they are withheld and listed as denied, D40): `started_at`, `ended_at` and the positions. It reads the `internal` personal-data fields masked. A person's token reads all of them in the clear.

### 5.8 Required kernel capabilities (Proposed)

**The decision for the first slice (2026-10-28, built, 8.6).** The capabilities below are not built as generic features of the kernel. Each rule is implemented inside the built-in module (guards, handlers and a read audit in `crates/modules/mileage`), so that it holds on REST, MCP and the command line, and nothing a caller can do through the generated `update`, `delete` or `bulk_create` gets round it. What was done, and what stays a generic capability for later, item by item:

| Item | Done inside the module (tested) | Stays a generic capability |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Write rights by state and field | The guard of `update` (`policy::writable_fields`): the driver writes seven fields while the trip is `recording`, `ended` or `rejected`, the reviewer `reject_reason` while it is `in_review`, nobody `state` or a server-set field; `delete` of a trip and `update` or `delete` of a point are refused; points are accepted only for the caller's own trip while it is `recording`; the guards of `create` and `bulk_create` start every trip as `recording` and refuse a state or a computed value in a row | A field `write_group`, `write_when` and `action_only` in a package (D79, Proposed). The back office still offers the buttons and fields the guards refuse (ISSUES M2) |
| 2 Read restriction by state | Guards on `get`, `list` and `search` of both models: a plain driver sees only their own records, a reviewer a trip from `in_review` on and its points, an administrator all; a hidden record answers like a missing one; aggregates follow the same rows | D79 `restrict` (Proposed) |
| 3 Separation of duties | `policy::check_action` in the guard and in the handler, with the approver as the decider | A caller-aware assertion (MODULES.md 3.8) |
| 4 Distance computation | The handler of `end` computes and stores the distance, the flags and the end position from the stored points, in the same write as the state | None: the helper is a Rust module function |
| 5 A retryable refusal | The code `incomplete` (409) in `errors::TABLE`; any module can return it with `errors::coded` | A way for a package assertion to name a code |
| 6 Retention job | Not built (ISSUES M5) | Required |
| 7 `confirmed_at` | The handler of `confirm` stamps it with the state | Not needed generically if a D56 effect can set `$now` on the record itself; not checked |
| 8 `if_version` | Not built; `update` still refuses it as an unknown field | Required before an outbox `update` must refuse a change made on an older state |

The text of the Proposed capabilities follows as it was written before the decision.

The design above assumes things the platform does not do today. Each is a requirement, not an assumption, and none is built:

1. **State-conditioned and field-level write rights.** Refuse the driver's `update` outside `recording`, `ended` and `rejected`; let the driver write only the fields of the table in 5.7; let the reviewer write `reject_reason`, and only while `in_review` (the reviewer holds no `write_group` of the model, and the model has one); make `integrity`, `distance_m`, `point_count`, `max_seq`, `confirmed_at` and `state` unwritable by `update`; refuse any `update` and `delete` of a point except by the retention job; refuse new points for a trip that has left `recording`. Today a package field's `group` gates only who may read it, and `[model.access]` (D58) decides which rows a person sees and defaults `own`, not what a state allows. D79 (Proposed) describes a field `write_group`, `write_when`, `action_only` and separate write and delete positions, and D77 immutability is the database-level guard. Whether the installed code accepts any of these in a package is not checked, and which of them carries this is not decided.
2. **A state-conditioned read restriction.** The reviewer reads trips and their points only from `in_review` on. D58 `see_all_group` shows every state; D79 `restrict` (Proposed) is the candidate; not checked.
3. **Separation of duties.** Refuse `confirm` and `reject` when the caller is the driver of the trip. Assertions (D56) see the record and its children, not the caller (MODULES.md 3.8).
4. **Distance computation.** A way for the geodesic helper to compute and store `distance_m` and the integrity flags from the accepted points, in the same transaction as the point batch (or with `end` waiting for the computation), so that a transient zero cannot refuse `end` or lead to a discarded trip; and to apply the speed cap and the accuracy filter.
5. **A retryable refusal.** A stable, machine-readable code for a failed assertion that the outbox can tell from a refusal for good (5.3, step 4). It is not checked whether a refusal already carries such a code.
6. **A retention job** that thins or erases points and their D75 history (5.6).
7. **A way to set `confirmed_at`** when `confirm` runs, for example an action effect (D56); not checked.
8. **The optimistic lock `if_version`** that D75 reserves, accepted by `update` (today it is refused as an unknown field), if an update of the outbox must refuse a change made on an older state of the trip. The first slice does not need it while updates only set the same values again.

### 8.4 The typed client `@obop/client` (built and run on 2026-10-28)

Step 2 of the first slice (9). Marking as in 8.3: **verified** means the session ran the command, and the commands are listed. Nothing was tried on a phone or an emulator. **What exists.**

- `obop openapi [--out FILE]` (`crates/obop/src/openapi_cmd.rs`) writes the OpenAPI 3.1 document of an instance without a server or a database. It builds the app in memory with the linked modules (base, product, crm, sales, invoicing, connectors, customize), takes an account that holds every permission group (so the document lists every command of every model that has a `list` command; seven commands of models without one are missing, see the gaps below) and runs the very function that `GET /api/openapi.json` runs, with the OAuth additions of a real instance. `servers` and `x-mcp` name the placeholder `http://localhost:8080`: a client takes the real address at run time. Keys are sorted at every level, the indent is two spaces, line ends are LF and the text ends with one newline. Packages installed on an instance later (the bundled marketplace catalogue, `mileage`) are not in it; a generic screen reads them from `GET /api/meta`, as the back office does, and a package may ship its own spec fragment (3).
- `web/packages/client/openapi.json` is that output, committed (867 KB). `web/packages/client/src/generated/schema.d.ts` is what `openapi-typescript` 7.13.0 makes of it, committed (697 KB; the header says it is generated and not to be edited).
- `@obop/client` (`web/packages/client`, private, Apache-2.0, ESM, TypeScript source in `exports` as `@obop/trip-core`): `createObopClient({ baseUrl, getToken, getLanguage?, languageParam?, fetch?, onUnauthorized? })` over `openapi-fetch` 0.17.0 with the generated `paths` (`client.api.POST("/api/party/list")` is typed from the schema); `call(model, command, input)` for any command, also of a model the schema does not know; `bulkCreate(model, rows, { idempotencyKey, mode, dryRun })`, typed from the model's writable fields (5.3 uses it for trips and points); `getMeta()` (typed loosely: the schema says only "The metadata."); one error class `ObopApiError` with `status`, `code`, `hint`, `assertKey` (the key of the assertion that refused, with `code` `assertion_failed`), `message`, `applied` (`none`, `unknown` or `partial`, from the body or `X-Obop-Applied`) and `retryAfterSeconds`; `ObopNetworkError` (status 0; `applied` is `none` for a read and `unknown` for a write) and `ObopApprovalRequired` as subclasses; and `isSafeToRetry(err, { idempotent })`. Its 503 rules are those of docs/DEPLOY.md "What a 503 means" (D74): `applied: none` can be repeated; `unknown` (and a 5xx with no word) only when the call carries an idempotency key or only reads ("read before you repeat a write"); `partial` never, with or without a key ("do not repeat a partial request blindly: read the records it touched and finish or undo the rest"; a keyed repeat of `bulk_create` returns the stored first answer and finishes nothing). Three rules are this client's own and are not in DEPLOY.md: a 429 can be repeated after `Retry-After` (it was refused before it ran), a 409 with the code `incomplete` can be repeated with backoff (D87; nothing was changed, and any other 409 is never repeated), and a lost connection is `none` for a read and `unknown` for a write. Every other status is never repeated. `parseRetryAfter` reads delta-seconds and the IMF-fixdate form of an HTTP date, treats anything else as absent and cuts a wait to one hour (`MAX_RETRY_AFTER_SECONDS`). A 401 calls `onUnauthorized` once (401s that arrive together share one run; a call that sent no token, such as a failed sign-in, does not call it) and rejects. Calls made while the handler runs, such as its own refresh through the same client, neither wait for it nor start it again: their 401 rejects like any other, or the handler would wait for itself. A success status whose body is not JSON, or is JSON of another shape (a website's page at a mistyped address), rejects with `ObopApiError` code `invalid_response` and the status the server sent, and is never repeated. The client never repeats a call itself. The runtime code uses the global `fetch`, `Request`, `Response` and `Headers` and nothing else (a test reads the sources for DOM and Node-only names). `getLanguage` is the language the app shows (D52 amendment 2026-10-28): it is asked for on every call and sent as `Accept-Language`, so a package's translated message of an assertion (`assertKey` names it) comes back in the language of the app and not of the device; a hook that throws, or a value that is no header, is skipped and never fails the call. The instance picks the language by `?lang=`, then the `obop_lang` cookie, then `Accept-Language`; a phone app sends no cookie, so the header decides. `languageParam: true` also puts `?lang=` on `call` and `bulkCreate`, for a web app on the instance's own origin.

cd web/packages/client && vp run generate:types                  # writes src/generated/schema.d.ts

**What the document says and does not say (read for this step).** The generated types are only as good as the document, which is written by hand in `crates/kernel/obop-http/src/openapi.rs`. These gaps are not fixed here; the client works around them:

- The record schemas have no `id`, though every record answered carries one.
- `GET /api/meta` is described only as "The metadata."; `ObopMeta` is written by hand.
- A generated operation declares 400, 401, 404, 503 and the 202 of a proposal. The error table (`errors.rs`) can also answer 403, 409 (`incomplete`), 410, 412 and 429 (`rate_limited` of `bulk_create`; sign-in lockout and the like come from other routes); the client maps every status the same way. ### 8.5 The mobile sign-in (built and run on 2026-10-28)

Step 3 of the first slice (9): the server side of the sign-in of a native app. Marking as in 8.3: **verified** means the session ran the command and the commands are listed below. Nothing was tried on a phone, an emulator or a system browser session of a real device, and no app uses any of it yet: the TypeScript sign-in helper of `@obop/client` (PKCE, the authorization URL, the exchange, the refresh and the storage) belongs to the app step. (This is the state of this step on its day: step 5 added the helper and the app signed in through a Chrome Custom Tab on an Android emulator, 8.7.) The `device` model is built under the name the spec uses; whether it is the `pos_device` record of the POS design (D28) stays open (15, item 2) and nothing here decides it.

**What exists.**

- `obop-oauth`: the client class (`obop_client_class` at registration, `mcp` when absent so every earlier client and test is unchanged; `mobile` otherwise) with the redirect rules of RFC 8252 (`redirect::validate_mobile`, `matches_mobile`), the `/api` resource (`api_resource`), the class carried through the authorization flow, the code and the grant, the consent texts of the mobile class in the seven languages of the pages, the `device` record written in the same atomic batch as the grant and the first refresh token, `last_seen` and the reported app version moved in the same batch as a rotation, `OAuth::revoke_device`, and the marking of the device by every way that ends a family (`oauth_*` revocation by the person or the application, reuse of a refresh token or a code, a lost account, the cleanup a week after the end).
- `obop-auth`: the access token of a mobile app (an `auth_token` of kind `agent` with the marker `mobile` and the device it was issued to, so that an older build reads it as a scoped agent that it refuses on every route), `Principal::is_person` and `Principal::device`, and the ending of devices with sessions on a credential change and on an offboarding, each device written to the log as it is ended.
- `obop-http`: the command routes accept the audience `<public URL>/api` (`HttpState::auth_api`); every other route still refuses a token that carries an audience.
- `obop-commands` (`devices`): the names, the shape of a record, the checks of what an app reports, `may_see`, the `DevicesApi` port. `obop-pkg` (`devices`): the model in the Customize module and its commands.
- The back office: a Devices table in Customize, Security (rename, revoke; administrators see everybody's), and the three device events in the security log.
- `docs/DEPLOY.md` "Mobile apps" is the operator's view and the analysis of what a malicious registration can do.

**Decisions made here, and why.**

1. **The class is a label of the client, set at registration.** Registration stays open (RFC 7591) and rate limited like every other; the class changes the rules for redirects and for what the token is, and grants nothing. A client that does not say is an MCP client.
2. **Redirects.** A private-use scheme must be in reverse domain form (at least two labels, lower case) and has a path and nothing else (RFC 8252 7.1), and `http` is allowed only to `127.0.0.1` and `[::1]` with a free port (7.3), never `localhost`. An `https` link (7.2) is **not** allowed: it belongs to the app only if the web site proves it, which the server does not check, and open registration would let a client stand a web page of its own behind our consent screen (the review of this step; verified links are a later item, ISSUES L9). The match is exact. A mobile client that registers `https://example.com/cb`, `http://example.com/cb`, `claude://callback` or `localhost` is refused. The consent page says what is true of the form: the app on this phone that registered the scheme, or a program on this computer.
3. **The token is the person, narrowed; it is not an agent token.** Reusing the agent token would have made every action the app's outbox queues for a driver (`discard`, `submit`) a proposal that waits for another person, which contradicts 5.2. The narrowing is the rule of D18: the groups the person ticked, intersected with what they hold at the moment of the request. The consequence is that the approval gates that hold an AI agent do not hold the app (ISSUES L2).
4. **A narrowed token is never a person (`Principal::is_person`).** Otherwise a one-hour mobile token could issue a never-ending agent token, change a password, enrol a second factor or approve a proposal. Every place that asked "is this a user principal?" for account management asks this now (the second-factor, passkey, account, agent-token and approval code paths), and the account routes, the approval route and the blob routes refuse an audience-bound token as before.
5. **A mobile app is offered only what it declared, and never an administrative or approving group.** A phone is lost. The client declares the groups it will ask for at registration (the `scope` of RFC 7591, at most 32); the page offers those that the person holds and that a phone may have, ticks none except those the app named in the `scope` of its request (which must be among those declared), and the server checks the decision again. The groups a phone may never have are `platform.*`, those that end in `.admin`, `_admin`, `.manager`, `.approver`, `_approver`, `.officer`, `.designer`, `.reviewer`, `.publisher`, `.preparer`, `.owner` or `_owner`, and the named list `obop_commands::devices::NEVER_FOR_MOBILE` (CONVENTIONS 4). A client that declares nothing is offered nothing and signs in with no group.
6. **The device is read at every request and fails closed.** One lookup by primary key per request made with a mobile token, no write. A device that is not `active`, is missing, or cannot be read (the store refuses) is no principal, so the request is refused with a 503 (never a 200). That makes the revocation independent of whether the token record could be written and of the hour of the token.
7. **`last_seen` moves at a refresh**, in the rotation batch, not at every request: a write for each request would be the main cost of the feature.
8. **The device metadata goes in the token request**, not in the authorization request: a browser URL is logged and kept in history, and the token request is a form body. It is checked before the code is spent, and read for a mobile client only.
9. **Ending a family ends the device and the other way round.** `device.revoke` calls the code that ends a connected application; the marking of the device and the revoking of the access token are both tried even if the first is refused. A credential change or an offboarding ends the device by the same step as the sessions, before them, so that a refused step leaves the sessions as live as the log says.
10. **Where the model lives.** The Customize module owns `device` (it already owns the security log and the Connected apps), the people who read their own devices need no group (`*` with the `own` rule of D58), and the one group created is `api.device_admin`, named after the existing `api.*` family. Reusing `platform.admin` alone would have given a helpdesk person the whole instance. The Connected apps table lists MCP clients only; a mobile app is listed as a device.
11. **The token on disk is an agent token with a marker.** `kind` is `agent`, `mobile` is `true` and `device` names the device. A build from before the class reads such a record as a scoped agent bound to an audience it refuses everywhere, not as a session of the person; with a kind of its own it would have read a full session. A test keeps the fixture of what that build does with the record.
12. **The device commands follow what kind of token calls (5.7).** The token of an app reads, renames and ends its own device and no other, and cannot list or search; an agent only proposes; the administrators read and end any and rename their own. The token response carries `device_id` so that the app can name its own device.
13. **A sign-in is bound to the credential epoch.** The epoch is read before the password is looked at, kept in the flow and the code, compared at the decision and before the code is spent, and compared again in the atomic write that creates the device and its family. A credential change that lands before the write fails the sign-in with no device and no token; one that lands after it ends the device with the sessions. A refresh does not compare it (a change of groups moves it too).
14. **At most 20 devices of one person** (`OAuthConfig::max_devices_per_user`): refused at the consent step and at the exchange, with the event `device_limit`.
15. **Text that hides text is taken out of what an app says.** A device name, platform and version have the characters that hide or reorder text removed (`devices::strip_hidden`, from the predicate `is_invisible`, which includes the blank-drawing characters such as the Braille blank and the Hangul fillers), white space becomes one space, and a zero-width joiner or non-joiner stays only between two characters that are seen, so a phone named with an emoji family or in Persian still signs in. A value with nothing visible left, or one that does not fit its field, is refused before the code is spent. The same function serves the rename, and the name of a client is stripped of the same characters (joiners included) at registration. The name of a device is a personal field and an AI agent reads it masked; the name of a client is plain text with those characters removed.
16. **The model name `device` is reserved** for packages (`RESERVED_MODELS`), and the refusal is in the one function every route registers a package through (`Installer::register`): a package that has a model of that name is not registered by the load at start, an upgrade, a rollback or the sync with another process, and keeps its records untouched.
17. **Who the log names.** The entry of an ended device names whoever ended it (the person, the administrator, the person who approved an agent's proposal, with the agent in the text), and a device that a credential change ends is written to the log before it is ended.

### 8.6 The Mileage backend (built and run on 2026-10-28)

A reviewer or an administrator cannot sort, group or measure the points by `lat`, `lon`, `accuracy_m`, `speed_mps` or `recorded_at`, nor the trips by a start or end position: those are read row by row, with the read logged. **Error codes.** `incomplete` (409, origin D87) in `errors::TABLE`, documented in docs/MODULES.md 6.2, returned by `end` only while more points could still arrive; and `rate_limited` (429 with `Retry-After: 60`, origin D70), the coded answer of the `bulk_create` limit, for every model. The two are retryable in `@obop/client` (`isSafeToRetry`: a 429 and a 409 `incomplete`; any other 409 is not).

**Log.** Two kinds of the security log: `mileage_route_read` (every `get`, `list` and `search` of `mileage_point` by a reviewer or an administrator, whatever the answer holds: one entry per driver whose points it returned, else one for the driver a filter on `trip` or `driver` names, else one for `*` (a count or a total over all drivers); the entry is written before the answer leaves, and a read whose entry cannot be written is refused) and `mileage_discard_stuck` (written in the same atomic batch as the discard). **What was built (the code, tests and builds were run on 2026-10-28; the run on an emulator is in "Verified on the emulator" below).** The app is `web/apps/mileage-app`; it takes its parts from `@obop/client`, `@obop/trip-core` and the new `@obop/ui-native`.

- **`src/core/`: pure TypeScript, no React Native import, tested with `vp test` (see `vp test`).** The outbox behind a `SqlDb` port (`sql-store.ts`: schema version 1 with the tables trip, point, batch and meta, WAL), the upload engine (`engine.ts`), the token session (`session.ts`: one refresh at a time however many calls fail with 401; a generation number that changes with every change of the sign-in, so that a refresh whose answer comes after a sign-out is dropped and writes nothing; a refresh that is given up after 60 seconds ends the sign-in as "unsure" and is not repeated with the token that may be spent, L29), the sync controller (`sync.ts`: when to run, the timers) and the typed calls (`api.ts`). The tests run the real SQL on `node:sqlite` and the engine against a fake instance (`src/test-support/fake-server.ts`) that can be told to fail on a given call, to answer 429, 503 (applied none, or applied unknown), to drop the connection, and to forget idempotency keys after 24 hours. The rules the engine follows are those of 5.3 and 8.6: a trip row is created with the key `trip:<client_trip_id>`; points go in fixed, immutable batches of at most 1,000 by `bulk_create` with `mode: all_or_nothing` and the key `points:<client_trip_id>:<first>-<last>` (the range is stored before the first send, so a retry sends the same rows under the same key); a 429 waits for `Retry-After`; a 503 and a lost connection are retried with the same key; a 401 tries one refresh and, if that fails, the app is signed out and the outbox is kept for the same account; a proxy or portal that answers in place of the server (`invalid_response`) and a 408 are repeated later like a 503; `end` is retried on 409 `incomplete` with a delay that starts at 8 seconds (four times the engine's 2 second base) and doubles up to 5 minutes (or the server's `Retry-After`), and from the fifth answer the trip screen says that the server is still missing points (the trip stays "Uploading", the phone keeps asking, and the driver can discard it; never a failure) and stops on a 400; a trip that the server has discarded, or says it does not have (the plain 400 "no mileage_trip X", with those words and no other sign: a 404 is not taken for it, because the server also sends 404 for a command that is forbidden or hidden, so after the create step the trip is kept with every point and flagged `unknown`, to try again and not to discard, for the points, the end, the submit and the read of a discard alike; at the create step a 404 keeps the server's own code and the trip may be discarded, which finishes on the phone: the phone holds no server id there, so a refusal is final for the create (if an earlier create had been applied and its answer lost, the server may still hold a trip, and the discard on the phone leaves it there; L30), is finished on the phone: its points are removed and a discard of it succeeds locally; the discard of a trip whose create was refused for good (the person may not use mileage any more) finishes on the phone too, because the server has no trip for it; `end`, `submit` and `discard` first read the trip from the server and do what is still missing, because a first answer may have been lost (M7); a trip whose key the server has forgotten (after 24 hours) is looked up by `client_trip_id` before it is created again. A pass remembers the sign-in it started under and stops before its next call when that changed, and the sync controller then starts a new pass under the new sign-in, so that the rest of the outbox of a signed-out person is never sent with the next person's token. The `client_trip_id` is a version 4 UUID from `expo-crypto`.

- **Whose trips the outbox holds, and what Sign out does (the review of PR 27, finding 1; `src/core/identity.ts`).** The outbox belongs to one person on one instance. After a sign-in the app asks the instance who owns its device record (`device.get`, the `user` field) and keeps `{origin, user}` in the meta table. A sign-in that finishes as the same person at the same address gets its trips back. A sign-in as **another person, or at another address,** finds the previous owner's unsent trips *foreign*: nothing is uploaded (the sync controller does not run, a pass that was already running stops before its next call, and the client refuses calls while the outbox is foreign), nothing is shown (one screen replaces all others: how many trips and points the phone holds, "Sign out and keep them", or "Delete them and continue" behind a second confirmation), and nothing is deleted without that choice; a trip of theirs that was still recording is stopped on the phone. The address is the canonical address the app signed in to, because the phone cannot know more (the instance's database id is not exposed over HTTP), so **the same person at a new address of the same instance is foreign too**: the trips are uploaded when the person signs in at the old address again, and 9.1 tells the tester to keep the PC's address (L39). A foreign outbox that holds nothing unsent is emptied and adopted. "Unsent" means the server does not hold the route: points still on the phone, work still to do, or a standing refusal, so a trip that a refusal stopped counts. **Sign out** and **Remove this phone** say how many trips are not completely on the server and how many points those trips hold on the phone (points that were uploaded already are counted when their trip is not finished on the server), and let the driver keep them for the same account (the default) or delete them (after a second confirmation); with nothing unsent the whole local copy is cleared, and when trips are kept everything else of that person stays on the phone with them, bound to its owner (their other trips as the list the server gave, without the route points of finished trips; it goes with "Delete them and continue" of a foreign sign-in); a trip that is open is stopped on the phone first. **Sign out** goes in this order (`signOut` and `leaveSession` in `native/actions.ts`): the request to the instance is started first, because it needs the refresh token and the app reads that before it forgets it; the phone does not wait for the answer to end its own side: the sync stops, an open trip is stopped, the unsent trips are counted, the tokens leave memory and the Keystore, and the trips are kept or deleted; the answer is waited for last, for 5 seconds at most (an earlier text of this document and a comment in the code said that the tokens leave first; that was not the order). When the instance could not be told (offline, or no answer in 5 seconds) the sign-in screen says so while it is open: the notice follows a flag, so it appears when the answer is in (up to 5 seconds after the screen), on every sign-out path, and it is gone for good when the screen is left (the first version read the flag once, when the screen opened, and showed the notice on one path only). The same notice is shown when the phone ended the sign-in itself after a refresh that had no answer (the "unsure" end, L29) and the revoke that it sends in the background fails too (third review round, `onEndUntold`; the text of the notice says "the sign-in is over on this phone", which fits both cases). **Remove this phone** goes the other way round: it asks the instance first (`device.revoke`, 30 seconds at most) and signs out only when that has answered; when the instance cannot be reached it throws, nothing is removed and the app stays signed in. When the sign-in ends **during a trip** (expiry; a device revoked by an administrator) the Recording screen stays, with a banner and **Stop** (Stop needs no connection and no sign-in); the points stay in the outbox and the driver may go on to the sign-in from there. If the database cannot be opened the screen offers Try again and stopping the location service (L34). Section 3 used to say that the database is "wiped when the device is revoked"; the app does not do that and the row now says what it does.

The recovery can be repeated: on iOS a key that was made is stored first as `pending:<key>` and becomes the plain key only after a database was opened with it, so a failed deletion of the old file, or a start of the process that is killed between the two steps, leaves a pending key that the next start recognises and recovers from (the first version stored the key at once, and every later start then failed on "file is not a database"; found by the review, tests for both orders). If anything after the open throws (dropping the sign-in of a fresh database, writing the note, or making the pending key plain), the opened file is closed before the error goes on, because the caller forgets a failed open and a retry would open the file a second time (found by the closure check; tests for each of the three causes). The recovery can be repeated, the note cannot; this is as it was in the first version of this change, and it is not changed. **Where the study was wrong or incomplete (found by running; the other statements of the study were not contradicted by the runs).**

1. *The Simulator and the Keychain.* The study said the Simulator "proves only that the option is passed". An unsigned Simulator build cannot use the Keychain at all (see Build), and a signed one shows the stored rows and their class: the Simulator proves more than the study thought. What it cannot show is a read while the phone is locked.
2. *The Failed screen named Android's way out.* "Settings, Apps, obop Mileage, Storage, Clear storage" was shown on iOS, where none of that exists. Found when the unsigned build showed the Failed screen; the study's list of Android words did not include it. It now has an iOS sentence (delete the app and install it again; unlock the phone once if it has only just started).
3. *The seven translation files.* The study asked for the same keys in each translation file. The app has English only: one catalogue, `src/i18n/en.ts`, and `LOCALE = "en"`. So there are no translation files to keep in step, and the app is not translated into the seven languages of the instance (translating it is not done; the Components bullet above says the same). `src/i18n/index.test.ts` checks the plural forms and the placeholders of that one catalogue and that each iOS sentence belongs to a sentence of it; it does not check languages.
4. *How to drive the UI.* The study's Simulator plan used `simctl location` and said nothing about tapping; `simctl` has no tap. The drive needed an XCUITest bundle, run with `xcodebuild test`. Five traps found on the way: `xcodebuild` cannot see devices of a custom `simctl --set` device set; a simulated route is cleared when a UI test session ends or another starts, so one session has to span the route, and the host starts the route when the test prints that the trip is recording; the keyboard's suggestion bar covers the lower half of the web page's password field, so the test taps near the top edge of the field; after the password is submitted iOS may offer to save it in a sheet over the app, and `dismissSavePasswordPrompt()` in `MileageDrive.swift` closes it (the sheet did not show in every run); `xcodebuild` hangs for about 10 minutes after a failed test, so the host stops it 90 s after the session's result.
5. *The thinning gap.* The study proposed about 3 s and said the number was to be fixed after the Simulator run. 2 s is what the port ships. The 1 Hz runs above are the measurement: on the synthetic route the rule cost from 0 up to about 1.2%, depending on a phase that nobody chose, and a real road was not measured.
6. *`NSBonjourServices`.* The first test build's built `Info.plist` still held `NSBonjourServices` (`_expo._tcp`) of the development client (the gate warned about it); the plain build's did not. The closure check showed a mechanism that fits, and it has nothing to do with test against plain: the strip phase of the development client removes the key in a clean build and does not in an incremental build with a plist that holds it, as set out in "Why the entries of the development client can reach a Release build" above. Whether the port session's first build was an incremental one was not recorded, so that this was the cause of its key is not established (this session had not looked into it). 7. *The Always keys.* The study's open question, whether a build without them is flagged, was not answerable on the Mac (nothing was uploaded). Nothing in the Simulator run asks for "Always".

**Open decisions for the lead and the owner.**

- **The thinning gap (`RECORDING_THINNING`).** A trip may hold 20,000 points. With one kept fix per 2 s that is reached after about 11 hours of continuous movement (after that the app stores no more points and the Recording screen says to stop the trip and start a new one); with no thinning at 1 Hz after about 5.5 hours. - **The bundle id of the test build and the store build** (Q37, DECISIONS.md).
- **The fallback if the iPhone test shows that a locked, pocketed iPhone stops delivering fixes under "When In Use"** (4.2): needs an owner ruling before any work.
- **`NSAllowsLocalNetworking` in a release build.** It is in every build (the template's). A release build still reaches a plain `http` address on the local network at the OS level if the app's own address rule were bypassed; the rule and the gate are the only guards.

**The reference route and the tolerance (write both down BEFORE the drive).** A closed loop that starts and ends at the same place, **at least 10 km and about 30 minutes** (the battery figure needs the 30 minutes). Draw it on a map with a tool that adds up a drawn path (put enough waypoints on it that it follows the roads you will drive) and write down its length in km to two decimals, with the date: this is the **reference length**. Write down the car's odometer at the start and the end (0.1 km) as well: a second figure that is reported and is not a limit. **The tolerance (Proposed): the distance that the server computes is within 5% of the reference length.** Why 5%: the drawn route differs from the driven one by lane choices, roundabouts and the ends (about 1 to 2%), the track is sampled about every 5 s so corners are cut a little (an estimate, not measured: how much depends on the number of turns and on the 5 s step, and no drive has measured it), a car standing at a red light makes the GPS wander, and the map tool has its own error; the 5% is a placeholder that the owner may widen before the drive. Do not change the number after the drive; if the result is outside it, the report says "failed against the tolerance" with both numbers and the owner decides whether the tolerance or the app is at fault. The check on the stored points does not depend on a tolerance: every point must be there once and in order.

**`OBOP_RECORD_THINNING=off` is for this first drive only.** The app then does not thin the fixes the iPhone delivers, instead of dropping a fix that is closer than 2 s and 50 m to the last stored one (`src/core/thin.ts`), so that the points on the server show what the phone delivers, and `vp run emulator:thinning` can replay the thinning rules over them afterwards (step D3.2). It is not "every fix": `appendFixes` still drops an implausible fix, a fix that is not later than the last stored one and a fix older than the trip's start by more than 10 s, and stops at 20,000 points. The app honours the variable only in a test build that also allows plain `http`, and `vp run ios:gate` **fails** a release build that carries it. A 30 minute drive at one fix per second is about 1,800 points, far under the 20,000 of a trip. Leave the variable out of every build that is not this drive.

## 10. Non-functional requirements

- **Security.** No secret in the app bundle; tokens only in the platform keystore; the device database with unsent points encrypted at rest with a key in the keystore (3); PKCE S256; the system browser only; access tokens are short-lived (one hour, for the MCP clients of D64 and for the mobile class alike) and bound to the `/api` audience (the D64 amendment, built: 8.5). A revoked device is refused at its next request (8.5).
- **Privacy.** 5.6. Every field of the package has a data class and a description (D41).
- **Reliability.** The outbox survives a killed app and a restart. A replay inside 24 hours is idempotent by the key of `bulk_create`; a later replay fails closed on the unique constraints (5.3). The server computes the distance.
- **Accessibility.** The app follows the intent of WCAG 2.2 AA (D35) with the platform screen readers (TalkBack on Android, VoiceOver on iOS); no gate is defined for it yet (Proposed). The checklist for a device pass (Proposed; every control has a role and a name that TalkBack or VoiceOver reads, focus order follows the visual order, touch targets are at least 48 dp on Android and 44 pt on iOS (Apple's guideline, general knowledge), text scales to 200%, nothing relies on colour alone, the recording state is announced) stays here. The human TalkBack pass is **not scheduled** (the owner, relayed 2026-10-28; OR-34 f) and is not a required step of the real-phone test. **The VoiceOver pass is not scheduled either**: OR-34 f names TalkBack only, and no owner ruling about VoiceOver is recorded (the iOS port, 8.8, did not try it). **Until they are done, the app's accessibility is unchecked on a real device**; no agent can certify TalkBack or VoiceOver. On iOS, which has no live regions, the banners announce a warning or a problem explicitly (8.8); only Home was looked at in the Simulator.
- **Performance.** No number is claimed: the battery drain over a 30-minute drive is measured on real phones (9, acceptance item 5).
- **Test plan.** `trip-core` and the shared vectors run under `vp test`; the Rust validator, the mobile class and the capabilities of 5.8 run under `cargo test` including the D74 fault rules; tests prove that a repeated batch inside 24 hours creates nothing, that a repeat after expiry is refused by the unique constraint, that `end` is refused while points are missing and succeeds when the count matches, that the driver cannot edit a `confirmed` trip or touch a point, that a reviewer cannot confirm their own trip, and that retention removes raw points from the table and from history; a Playwright test in `web/packages/e2e` checks that the back office shows a trip in `in_review` and confirms it; a Rust test and the same Playwright suite check that revoking a device ends its refresh-token family and that the app is refused at the next call; the acceptance test of 9 runs on a real phone; all data is synthetic.

## 11. Where obop deliberately differs from Odoo

Odoo's mobile apps are Enterprise. obop has one edition under Apache-2.0 and no `ee` tree (OR-1, OR-2), so a mobile client is never a paid add-on. obop does not plan a suite of native apps: the PWA stays the default client, and D87 proposes that a native app exists only where a PWA cannot do the job (Proposed).

## 12. Out of scope, non-goals and later

- Auto-stop until the sign-off of Q41 exists. Automatic start is not ruled by OR-34 or Q41; it is out of scope for the first slice (manual start is the baseline), and it would also need the background-location permission and the Play declaration (4.2). Geofences and car Bluetooth triggers belong to automatic start.
- Continuous GPS monitoring of staff. A trip is recorded only while a driver has started it, and the route is audited when read.
- Tauri mobile, a Capacitor app, an own native location module (unless the spike fails), and the Transistorsoft library in the default build.
- Maps, reverse geocoding and tile servers (Q39); push (Q38); native passkey prompts; the store steps of iOS (privacy label, privacy manifest, export compliance, listing; the iOS port itself is 8.8); reimbursement and expense lines (5.9); a month lock; a review screen in the app; private-trip route storage.
- Apple and Google accounts, store listings and publication by an agent (Q37).

## 15. Open questions

Owner questions are Q37 to Q43 in [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) section 8 (Q43, Google Play services, came with the Expo spike). Questions that this document leaves open and that no register row covers yet:

1. **Client package name.** `@obop/client` (the proposal) or the Proposed `@obop/rpc` of ARCHITECTURE 10.2; one package should serve the web and the app. Not decided here, and not decided by 8.4: the package that exists is named `@obop/client` because the layout of 8.1 says so, and renaming it later is a one-line change in each `package.json` that depends on it.
2. **Device model.** CONVENTIONS section 9.2 proposes kiosk and POS devices as API-client principals plus a `pos_device` record (D28). Whether `device` and `pos_device` are one model or two is open; nothing in this document or the glossary decides it, and the build of 8.5 (the model is called `device`, as here) decides nothing either.
3. **Interim data class.** `confidential_hr` for point fields until Q40 is answered (5.7).
4. **Private trips.** Keep only the distance, or drop the trip.
5. **Numbers.** The accuracy threshold, the open-trip hours, the retention days, the distance tolerance and the speed cap are open or Proposed.
6. **Reviewer scope.** All trips (the first slice), or only a manager's reports (the organisation grants of D79).
7. **Review in the app.** Whether a review screen is added to the app after the first slice (the first slice reviews in the back office).
8. **Permission groups and wire ids.** `mileage.driver`, `mileage.reviewer`, `mileage.admin`, `client_trip_id` and the action names are working names until CONVENTIONS sections 3 and 4 register them. `api.device_admin` (built in 8.5) is listed in CONVENTIONS 4 as Proposed.
9. **The Play question.** Whether a foreground service started from a visible app, or a trip recording of hours that a driver starts, avoids the background-location declaration (4.2). The policy text read on 2026-10-27 does not settle it.
10. **Correcting a confirmed trip.** Whether `confirmed` may return to `in_review` (an action of the administrator), or only a new trip corrects it.
11. **Month lock.** When designed: D77 immutability or a `mileage_month` model, the date field and zone that assign a trip to a month, and the rule for a trip confirmed late into a locked month.
12. **Separation-of-duties policy.** The explicit policy under which a business lets a reviewer confirm their own trip, if it does.
13. **The kernel capabilities of 5.8.** Decided for the first slice (2026-10-28): the rules are inside the built-in module (5.8, 8.6). Which of them become generic features, and which record or decision carries each, is open.
14. **Google Play services.** `expo-location` on Android depends on `com.google.android.gms:play-services-location` (8.3): a library that is not OSI-licensed and needs Google services on the phone. Whether that is accepted under the Apache-2.0-compatibility rule for dependencies and for phones without Google services, or whether the own-module fallback of 4.1 uses the platform `LocationManager`, is owner question Q43 in [DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md) section 8. It is needed before any store release. What the installed `expo-location` 58.0.18 does (8.7): the default entry, the one the app imports, needs Play services at run time; its `expo-location/next` entry has a platform task consumer that was not tried; `play-services-location` stays declared as `api` in both cases, so the licence part of the question stays either way.
