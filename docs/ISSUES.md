**Races, with counts.** `code_take` reads the code and then deletes it, and only the delete is the claim (row M11b: the result of the delete ignored). At least one of the two race tests of `obop-auth` failed in each of 10 of 10 runs of `cargo test -p obop-auth --lib oidc`; `eight_racers_for_one_code_leave_exactly_one_winner_in_every_round` (eight threads released by a barrier, 300 rounds) alone in 10 of 10 runs; the two-server test alone (two threads, now released by a barrier) in 6 of 10; the PostgreSQL test (ten rounds, a barrier, `OBOP_TEST_POSTGRES_URL` set to a postgres:18) alone in 5 of 5. The review measured the two-thread test without a barrier at 3 of 20. The mutation of `take_flow_from` (E1) is killed by its own barrier test.

**Timing tests.** The tests in `crates/obop/src/sso_tests.rs` that pin the limit of a call and the eight places for waiting do not wait for a guess of how long a busy machine takes. The stand-in holds a request until the test lets go of it (`Idp::hold`, `Idp::release`), the server tells a test how many requests wait (`HttpState::sso_waiting_for_test`), and a test lets go of the fetch it holds when the thing it pins has happened: eight wait and the ninth has been told; eight exchanges are in the air and the late fetch was refused. The limit is shortened while the exchange before the call under test is in the air, so that it is the limit of that call alone, and the test world runs with a 30 s limit unless a test sets its own. Before this, on a machine at 99 percent CPU with other builds running (32 logical processors), 3 of 8 runs of `cargo test -p obop --bin obop sso_tests` failed, and the first run of the next series failed in five tests; after, 12 of 12 runs passed, and 6 of 6 more with 64 spinning processes added (`3.2 s` to `7.0 s` per run). One more test failed once for another reason, `flow_round_trip_ends_in_a_bearer_token_and_keeps_nothing_of_the_provider`: it looked for the three letters `eyJ` anywhere in the log and the database, and they turn up by chance in the base64 text of a stored value of an account (it failed once, naming `auth_user/root1`); it looks for `.eyJ` now, the dot that follows the header of a token.
